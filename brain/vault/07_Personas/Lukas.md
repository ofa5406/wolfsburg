# Lukas

Persona 03: **Lukas, 13, secondary school student** — the persona who names what car dependency does to the people who cannot drive.

Before: everything beyond his neighbourhood depended on whether a parent could drive him. After: an e-bike from the nearest S-hub — faster than the old bus. **For the first time, the whole city feels like his.**

Lukas carries the freedom argument, aimed directly at the claim that car reduction restricts liberty ([[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]]): in a car city, a teenager is *less* free — mobility is rationed through an adult's schedule. The fine S-hub grain (a dock within 150–250 m of practically every front door) is what makes his independence real infrastructure rather than sentiment ([[S-Hub]], [[Service Radius]], [[Walking Catchment]]).

He is also the demographic tailwind personified: the generation already licensing later and owning less, for whom hub-based mobility is not a sacrifice but the obvious default ([[Young Germans Drive Less]]). Sabine's story shows the same shuttle from the parent's seat ([[Sabine]], [[Bike Sharing]]).
