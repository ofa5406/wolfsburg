# Street Connectivity

The principle that **a hub connects both sides of the street** — it is never marooned on one pavement. Three strategies apply depending on street width and traffic, combinable and mixable across the faces of a corner hub: the stone field crosses the carriageway as one continuous surface ([[Hub-Integrated Crossing]]); the carriageway is raised or narrowed into a traffic-calming moment ([[Raised Narrowed Carriageway]]); or elements distribute across both sides with the crossing as the link ([[Both-Sides Distribution]]).

The strategies encode a quiet radicalism: the hub treats the street as part of its floor plan, not as its boundary. Every hub thereby doubles as a pedestrian crossing and a calming device — 68 places where the carriageway concedes priority — which stitches the hub network into the walking network at exactly the points where transfers happen.
