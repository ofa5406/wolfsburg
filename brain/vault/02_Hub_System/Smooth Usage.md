# Smooth Usage

The design principle that the spatial arrangement should guide users **without additional signage**. Directional lines in the stone field, the positions of elements, and the three-zone logic create a legible sequence that works intuitively in both directions of use — arriving by vehicle or on foot, the hub explains itself through its own geometry and materials.

The principle is a bet on environmental legibility over instruction: signs are admissions that the space failed to communicate. It shapes concrete choices — wayfinding embedded in the paving rather than posted beside it ([[Directional Lines]]), information concentrated at one threshold rather than scattered, docks positioned where the approach path naturally delivers you. Smooth usage is also an accessibility strategy: a hub that works without reading works for children, tourists, and anyone the signage would have excluded.
