# Catalyst Not Shelter

The fifth design principle and the typology's ambition statement: **hubs are not transit shelters — they are new anchors of public life.** Environmentally generous (trees, planting, ecological infrastructure), socially generous (seating, shelter, social elements), spatially generous (quality public space, programme). Mobility is the anchor; social and cultural life is the ambition.

The principle guards against the gravitational pull of value engineering, which would happily shrink every hub to a dock and a sign. It justifies the "unproductive" elements — game tables, book exchanges, wildflower strips, art markers — as the actual point rather than decoration: a hub that people visit without travelling is a hub whose eyes-on-the-street never close. It is the principle most directly at stake in the [[Hub as Place]] versus [[Hub as Infrastructure]] debate, and the reason the palette's social category exists at all.
