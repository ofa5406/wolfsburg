# Hub-Integrated Crossing

The crossing absorbed into the hub: the reddish stone field continues across the carriageway, directional lines guide the crossing, and the hub reads as **one piece spanning both sides of the street**. Standard at S-hubs where street context requires it; the first of the three [[Street Connectivity]] strategies.

The element inverts the usual hierarchy at the kerb. A conventional crossing is the street graciously admitting pedestrians; a hub-integrated crossing is the hub's floor asserting itself over the street, with the material change itself doing the traffic-calming — drivers read the stone as someone else's territory. It is the smallest-scale instance of the project's core move: public space taking ground back from the carriageway, one hub-width at a time.
