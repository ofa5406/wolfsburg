# Sponge City

The water agenda hiding inside the parking agenda. **Sponge city** — the Chinese national programme launched 2014–15, championed by landscape architect Kongjian Yu — designs urban ground to **absorb, store and slowly release stormwater**: permeable surfaces, bioswales, retention parks and restored wetlands replacing the pipe-and-pave reflex. The same toolkit runs in the West as SUDS (sustainable drainage) and green infrastructure.

Its intersection with the project is literal: **parking is the enemy surface** — large, impermeable, heat-absorbing asphalt sheets that flash-flood the drains and cook the block ([[Car-Dedicated Land]], [[Urban Heat Island]]). Every reclaimed lot is de-pavement opportunity: Barcelona's superblock greening and the "sponge function" of converted parking are the documented pattern ([[Superblock Street Reclaim Percentages]], [[Reclaimed Land]]).

The hub palette carries the sponge kit natively — [[Permeable Surface Zone]], [[Planting Bed and Bioswale]], [[Tree with Stone Grate]], [[Wildflower Meadow Strip]] — so the mobility network doubles as distributed climate infrastructure: 68 small sponges where 68 drains used to flood ([[Copenhagen Mobility Hubs]]).
