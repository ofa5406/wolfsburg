# Fundamental Law of Road Congestion

The theory behind the project's oldest evidence. Anthony Downs proposed it in 1962 as the "law of peak-hour expressway congestion": **new capacity fills to the previous level of congestion**, because faster roads attract trips from other routes, other times, other modes — and, in time, new trips entirely. Duranton and Turner's 2011 econometric study gave it teeth: across US cities, **vehicle-kilometres travelled rise roughly one-for-one with lane-kilometres built** — an elasticity near 1. You cannot build your way out; the road *is* the traffic ([[Induced Demand]]).

The law's mirror is the project's friend: capacity *removal* un-induces demand — trips retime, re-route, re-mode or dissolve, which is traffic evaporation observed across seventy cases ([[Traffic Evaporation]], [[Disappearing Traffic Study]], [[LTN 46 Percent Internal Traffic Drop]]).

Together the pair forms one symmetric principle the boards can state in a sentence: **traffic is not a fixed quantity to be accommodated but a variable produced by the space offered to it** ([[Street-Space Reallocation]], [[Braess Paradox]]).
