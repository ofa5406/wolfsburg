# Etymology of Kiosk

A word that travelled from Persian garden to exhibition screen. **Kiosk** begins as Persian *kūshk* — pavilion, palace — passes into Turkish as *köşk*, the airy garden pavilion of Ottoman palaces (still the word for "villa" in modern Turkish), and enters French as *kiosque* in the 17th century when European gardens borrowed the form.

The 19th century urbanised it: Paris's *kiosques à journaux* (newspaper kiosks, from 1857) and bandstand *kiosques à musique* made the kiosk **the smallest unit of public architecture** — a roofed point of exchange in the street. The 20th century shrank it to telephone kiosks; the 21st abstracted it to self-service screens ([[History of the Kiosk]]).

The project uses the word at both ends of its history: the [[Vendor Kiosk Slot]] returns the built kiosk to the hub as micro-commerce, and the exhibition "kiosk" — the unattended screen at Summaery — is the same idea dematerialised: a small public interface where the city offers itself ([[Summaery Exhibition]], [[Hub as Place]]).
