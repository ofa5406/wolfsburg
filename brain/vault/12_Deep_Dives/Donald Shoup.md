# Donald Shoup

The economist who found the city's hidden code in its parking rules. Donald Shoup (1938–2025), UCLA professor of urban planning, published *The High Cost of Free Parking* in 2005 — 700+ pages arguing that **parking is not a background condition but the invisible subsidy that manufactures the car-dependent city**. Free for ~99% of US trips, mandated by minimums whose "science" is circular, consuming dwelling-sized land per space ([[Parking Free for 99 Percent of Trips]], [[Parking Minimums Circular Logic]], [[61 Square Metres per Parking Space]]).

His three reforms became a global movement (his followers self-describe as "Shoupistas"): **abolish parking minimums; price the kerb right; return the revenue to the neighbourhood.** Cities from Buffalo to São Paulo have since scrapped minimums ([[Parking Reform]]).

The project is Shoupian at its foundation: hubs are, in his terms, parking demand *collectivised and priced* instead of mandated and free — and the [[Car-Land Composite Map]] is his ledger drawn for one German city ([[Parking City]]).
