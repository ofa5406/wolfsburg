# Third Place

The sociology of where community actually happens. Ray Oldenburg (*The Great Good Place*, 1989) named the **third place**: neither home (first) nor work (second), but the café, barbershop, pub, square — neutral ground where presence is voluntary, status is levelled, conversation is the activity, and regulars set the tone. Third places, he argued, are where community is *manufactured*; suburbs fail socially because zoning and car dependence strip them out ([[Car Dependency]], [[Public Life]]).

The relevance to a mobility project is structural: transit stops are classically *non-places* — endured, not inhabited. The hub programme is an explicit attempt to make the mobility node a third place: the kiosk and café ([[Gastronomy and Permanent Programme]]), game tables ([[Game Table]]), generous seating ([[Group Seating]]), the book exchange ([[Micro-library Book Exchange]]) — regulars, neutral ground, levelled status, at the point everyone already passes ([[Hub as Place]], [[Catalyst Not Shelter]]).

Oldenburg supplies the test the exhibition can quote: a hub succeeds when people **stay who don't need to travel** ([[Agora]]).
