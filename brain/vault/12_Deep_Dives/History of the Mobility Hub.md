# History of the Mobility Hub

How the project's central concept assembled itself. The ingredients are old — the railway station forecourt, the tram interchange, the Dutch station bicycle garage — but the **mobility hub** as a named type is young: Bremen's *mobil.punkt* (2003) is the recognised first — car-sharing given kerb infrastructure at transit stops ([[Bremen mobil.punkt]], [[History of Car Sharing]]).

The 2010s multiplied the ingredients (bike-share, e-scooters, EV charging, apps) and the type spread as the container for them: Bergen's mobilpunkt (2018, the name itself borrowed), Vienna's WienMobil, Munich's MVG stations, Minneapolis's pilots ([[Bergen Mobilpunkt]], [[Vienna WienMobil]], [[Minneapolis Mobility Hub Pilot]]). CoMoUK's definition, typology and accreditation (from ~2019) and the SUMC's "where people go to move" gave it standards and theory ([[800 Metre Catchment Standard]], [[Hub Component Ladder]]).

The project's contribution sits at the concept's open frontier: every existing programme *adds* hubs to a car city; `<stadt.hub>` makes the hub the **organising element that replaces parking** — the type promoted from amenity to urban structure ([[Mobility Hub]], [[From Parking City to Hub City]], [[Hub as Infrastructure]]).
