# Salvador Rueda

The systems thinker behind the superblock. Salvador Rueda (b. 1953), ecologist and psychologist, founded Barcelona's Urban Ecology Agency and spent three decades developing the **supermanzana**: bundle ~3×3 blocks, route through-traffic around the perimeter, and convert the interior street network to living space at walking speed ([[Superblock]], [[Barcelona Superblocks]]).

Rueda's contribution was to make the superblock a *quantified* instrument, not an urban-design gesture: his agency computed noise, air, green access and mobility budgets for the whole of Barcelona, showing the model could scale to 500+ superblocks while keeping the city functional — the calculations behind the famous health-dividend numbers ([[Barcelona Health Dividend]]). The first full pilots ran in Vitoria-Gasteiz and Poblenou; Eixample followed.

His deeper frame travels to Wolfsburg: the street network as an *ecosystem budget* — a fixed spatial resource reallocated among competing uses — which is exactly the accounting the [[Car-Land Composite Map]] performs, and the logic by which the five zones spend their reclaimed interior ([[Five-Zone Model]], [[Street-Space Reallocation]]).
