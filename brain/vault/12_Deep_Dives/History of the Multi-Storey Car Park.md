# History of the Multi-Storey Car Park

The biography of the building type the L-hubs are made from. Purpose-built car storage is as old as the car itself: early 1900s "garages" were serviced warehouses with attendants and lifts; the ramp-access multi-storey matured in 1920s–30s America (Chicago's 1918 Hotel La Salle garage is a claimed first) and exploded across rebuilding Europe in the *autogerechte* decades — every German centre acquiring its concrete decks as civic equipment ([[Autogerechte Stadt]]).

Architecturally the type is defined by what it lacks: no daylight requirements, no insulation, generous spans, sloped or split floors — a building of pure structure, sized for machines. Which is exactly why its afterlife works: the frames accept housing, offices, farms and mobility programmes once the cars leave — the conversion wave Karparc leads ([[Newcastle Karparc]], [[Adaptive Reuse]]).

Wolfsburg's six central examples are the project's founding resource ([[L-Hubs from Car Parks Decision]], [[L-Hub]]): monuments of the parking city, structurally waiting to anchor the hub city ([[From Parking City to Hub City]]). The type's history ends, fittingly, as armature.
