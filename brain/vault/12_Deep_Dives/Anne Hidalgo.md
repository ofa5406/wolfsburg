# Anne Hidalgo

The politician who proved delivery survives elections. Anne Hidalgo (b. 1959), Mayor of Paris since 2014, turned Moreno's proximity theory into a capital city's programme ([[Carlos Moreno]]): the Plan Vélo's protected network, the pedestrianised Seine banks, the commitment to remove ~70,000 on-street parking spaces, 500 streets voted car-free ([[Paris 15-Minute City]]).

Her significance in the brain is the political lesson, not the infrastructure: she expanded the programme through loud, well-funded opposition — and **won re-election in 2020 by a landslide**, campaigning on more of it. Delivery, then consent: the same sequence as Ghent's re-elected alderman and Stockholm's post-trial referendum ([[Ghent Circulation Plan]], [[Stockholm Congestion Charge]], [[Imposition Without Consent]]).

For a project that must convince a German company town, Hidalgo is the standing counter-example to "car-reduction is political suicide": the largest such programme in Europe was *rewarded* at the ballot box, because its benefits arrived visibly and fast ([[Push Beats Pull Alone]], [[Risk — Company Town Politics]]).
