# History of the Superblock

One word, two opposite histories — the project uses the second. The **modernist superblock** (Radburn 1929, CIAM planning, Soviet microrayons, postwar estates) enlarged the block to *serve* the car: wide perimeter roads, interior separated from traffic by design, the street dissolved ([[Radburn Layout]]). Its failures — dead perimeters, placeless interiors — discredited the word for a generation.

The **Barcelona superblock** (Rueda's *supermanzana*, piloted from the 1990s in Born and Gràcia, tested in Vitoria-Gasteiz, scaled in Poblenou 2016 and the Eixample plan) inverted the recipe: keep the historic fine grain, but treat a 3×3 group of ordinary blocks as one unit whose *interior streets* are freed from through-traffic — the street not dissolved but **reclaimed** ([[Salvador Rueda]], [[Barcelona Superblocks]], [[Superblock]]).

Vienna's Supergrätzl translated the second model into German-speaking practice ([[Vienna Supergrätzl]]). The lineage note exists to keep the project on the right branch: Wolfsburg's zones enlarge the *filter*, never the block — Groningen's sectors and Rueda's interiors, not Radburn's exile ([[Five-Zone Model]], [[Filtered Permeability]]).
