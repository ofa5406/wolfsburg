# Colin Buchanan

The insider who named the ceiling. Sir Colin Buchanan (1907–2001), British planner and engineer, led the UK government study *Traffic in Towns* (1963) — commissioned to plan *for* mass motorisation, and famous for admitting what its sponsors did not want to hear: **a city cannot give the car everything it demands without destroying its own environmental quality**. Full accommodation would require rebuilding cities beyond recognition; short of that, traffic must be *limited by design*.

The report introduced the vocabulary of "environmental areas" — districts protected from through-traffic, served by a coarser distributor network — which is filtered permeability's respectable grandfather, drawn by the establishment a decade before Delft or Groningen made it street reality ([[Filtered Permeability]], [[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]]).

In the project's lineage Buchanan marks the moment car-capacity limits entered mainstream planning ([[Post-Car City]]): even the car's own engineers, doing the arithmetic honestly, concluded the parking city has a ceiling ([[Car Dependency]]).
