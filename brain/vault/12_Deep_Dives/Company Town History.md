# Company Town History

The urban type Wolfsburg belongs to, with its recurring plot. **Company towns** — settlements built and dominated by a single employer — run from paternalist model villages (Saltaire, Bournville, Krupp's Essen colonies) through Pullman, Illinois (1880s: the sleeping-car company's total town, broken by the 1894 strike) to Ford's Amazonian folly **Fordlândia** (1928: rubber, ruins by 1934) — and Wolfsburg, the type's largest European instance ([[Wolfsburg]], [[Wolfsburg Privileged Identity Study]]).

The recurring plot matters to the project: company towns flourish while the company's product ascends, and face an **identity crisis when the product turns** — the town's economy, politics and self-image being a single bet. Detroit is the automotive case at metropolitan scale.

Wolfsburg's fork in the plot is the project's opening: the company is *not leaving* — it is pivoting, from car-seller to mobility provider ([[NEW AUTO Strategy]], [[VW Cuts 35000 Jobs]]) — and a company town is uniquely able to pivot *with* its company: one landowner, one workforce, one decision structure ([[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]). The type's weakness (monoculture) becomes, for one transition, its speed ([[Employer-Anchored Commuting]]).
