# Etymology of Autonomy

**Autonomy** is Greek civics before it is robotics: *autos* (self) + *nomos* (law) — **the condition of giving oneself one's own law**. Greek cities were *autonomos* when they governed themselves; Kant made autonomy the core of moral personhood; only in the late 20th century did engineering borrow it for machines that operate without external control.

The borrowing hides a precise irony the project keeps visible. The "autonomous" vehicle is the *least* self-governing artefact on the road — bound to its geofence, its remote supervision, its operational design domain ([[Phased Autonomy]], [[Vehicle Automation Levels]]). Meanwhile the *car itself* was sold for a century as the buyer's autonomy — freedom as private machinery ([[German Car Culture]], [[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]]).

The project's implicit argument is an etymological correction: real autonomy belongs to *persons*, and the measure of a mobility system is whose self-law it enlarges — the teenager who no longer needs a chauffeur, the woman of 85 who reaches the market unaided ([[Lukas]], [[Gertrude]]). Machines get automation; people get autonomy.
