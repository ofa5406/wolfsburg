# Etymology of Stadt

The German word for city is a place to **stand**. *Stadt* descends from Old High German *stat* — "place, location" — from the Proto-Germanic and ultimately Proto-Indo-European root ***steh₂-**, *to stand*: the same root as English *stead* (homestead, instead), *stand* and *state*, and Latin *status*. A Stadt is, at bottom, *the standing-place* — where people stopped moving and stayed.

The doublet survives in modern German: *Statt* (stead, place) and *Stadt* (city) split from one word, and *stattfinden* — "to take place" — still says that events *find a standing-place*.

For a project named `<stadt.hub>`, the etymology is a quiet thesis statement: the city is the place of *staying*, and the last century let the car — the machine of *going* — dictate the form of the standing-place ([[Parking City]], [[Car-Dedicated Land]]). Rejoining *Stadt* to *hub* names the repair: movement concentrated at nodes so the ground between them can be stood upon, dwelt in, stayed ([[Hub City]], [[Public Life]], [[Etymology of Hub]]).
