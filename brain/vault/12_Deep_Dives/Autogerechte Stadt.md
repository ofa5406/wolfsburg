# Autogerechte Stadt

The German doctrine the project unbuilds. ***Die autogerechte Stadt*** — "the car-appropriate city" — is the title of Hans Bernhard Reichow's 1959 book and the name of the planning consensus that rebuilt postwar West Germany: cities reorganised around flowing traffic — widened arterials, ring roads, pedestrians lifted onto bridges or sunk into underpasses, the street's other functions cleared for the car. What bombs had opened, traffic engineering filled.

Wolfsburg is the doctrine's purest expression — not retrofitted to the car but **founded for it** ([[History of Wolfsburg]], [[Parking City]]): generous roads, dispersed districts, storage everywhere; the autogerechte Stadt built from a blank sheet.

Germany's own planning culture abandoned the term decades ago — it survives only as an epithet — yet its physical legacy is exactly the fabric the five zones, the sections and the hub network now rework ([[Street-Space Reallocation]], [[Five-Zone Model]]). The project names it deliberately: `<stadt.hub>` is the autogerechte Stadt's precise antonym — the *menschengerechte* correction, argued in the doctrine's model city ([[Post-Car City in VW's Hometown]]).
