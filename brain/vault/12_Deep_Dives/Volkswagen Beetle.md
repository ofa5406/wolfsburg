# Volkswagen Beetle

The car the city was built to build. The Beetle — Ferdinand Porsche's KdF-Wagen design of the 1930s, mass-produced in Wolfsburg from 1945 under the British occupation's restart of the plant — became the **best-selling single car design in history: over 21.5 million units**, the machine of the Wirtschaftswunder and the literal product the city's streets, halls and paycheques were organised around ([[History of Wolfsburg]], [[Volkswagen]]).

Its meaning in the brain is the *promise it embodied*: the people's car — affordable private mobility for every household — was the twentieth century's genuinely emancipatory offer, and Wolfsburg delivered it to the world. The project's argument gains honesty by conceding this: car dependency is the *success* of that promise, compounded until it consumed the city that made it ([[Car Dependency]], [[German Car Culture]]).

The narrative arc closes with the plant's next people's vehicle: the ID. Buzz AD — shared, electric, autonomous — as the Beetle's structural successor: again a VW vehicle proposing to democratise mobility, this time without requiring ownership ([[ID Buzz AD Production Level 4]], [[Access over Ownership]]).
