# Carlos Moreno

The brander of proximity. Carlos Moreno (b. 1959), Colombian-French professor at IAE Paris–Sorbonne, introduced the **15-minute city** in 2016: neighbourhoods where daily needs — work, shops, care, learning, leisure — sit within a 15-minute walk or ride ([[15-Minute City]]). Beneath the slogan is his **chrono-urbanism**: organising the city around time and proximity rather than speed and distance, through four principles — proximity, diversity, density, ubiquity ([[Chrono-Urbanism]], [[Proximity Planning]]).

His decisive partnership was political: as advisor to Anne Hidalgo, the concept became Paris's governing programme and, via C40, a global template ([[Paris 15-Minute City]], [[Anne Hidalgo]]). His 2024 book carries Gehl's foreword — the lineage closing its circle ([[Jan Gehl]]).

The concept also drew conspiracy theories framing it as movement restriction; the honest rebuttal — it is about *access*, never confinement — is part of the project's prepared answers ([[15-Minute City Conspiracy]]). For Wolfsburg, Moreno supplies the demand side of the thesis: hubs remove the need to own, proximity removes the need to travel far.
