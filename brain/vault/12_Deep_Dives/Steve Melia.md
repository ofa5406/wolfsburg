# Steve Melia

The namer of the project's network principle. Steve Melia, transport and planning academic at the University of the West of England, coined **filtered permeability** (in work culminating around 2012): the insight that a street network's openness should be *asymmetric by mode* — fully permeable for walking and cycling, deliberately filtered for through motor traffic ([[Filtered Permeability]]).

The naming mattered because the practice predated the theory: Delft's woonerven, Groningen's sectors and Houten's ring-fed segments had been *doing* filtered permeability for decades ([[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]], [[Houten]], [[History of the Woonerf]]). Melia's term gave the scattered practice a transferable principle — and a defensible answer to the accusation of being "anti-car": the filter removes only the *through* function, never access ([[Modal Filter]]).

His broader work on carfree development and the politics of traffic reduction (*Urban Transport Without the Hot Air*, 2015) feeds the project's honesty about backlash: he documents both the evidence and the opposition ([[Low-Traffic Neighbourhood]], [[Imposition Without Consent]]).
