# SAE Automation Levels

The taxonomy underneath every autonomy claim. SAE International's standard **J3016** (first issued 2014) defines six levels: **0** (no automation) through **1–2** (driver assistance — the human always drives, however much the marketing implies otherwise), **3** (conditional — the system drives, a human must take over on request), **4** (high — the system drives fully *within a defined operational design domain*, no takeover needed), to **5** (full — anywhere, any conditions, a category with no deployed example).

The load-bearing concept is the **operational design domain (ODD)**: Level 4 is not "almost 5" but a different philosophy — competence guaranteed inside a fence. Waymo is L4 in mapped cities ([[Waymo 450000 Weekly Rides]]); the ID. Buzz AD is L4 on defined services ([[ID Buzz AD Production Level 4]]); Level 5 remains, per the project's honest reading, undated ([[AV Timelines Keep Slipping]]).

The Hub City is engineered *as an ODD*: fixed hub-to-hub links, a mapped corridor, pre-positioned waves — the easiest fence autonomy could ask for, which is the technical core of the phasing strategy ([[Phased Autonomy]], [[Vehicle Automation Levels]], [[Commuter Corridor]]).
