# Complete Streets

The American policy cousin of the project's street sections. **Complete Streets** — the term coined in 2003 by US advocates, now adopted in over a thousand jurisdictions — requires streets to be designed for *all* users and ages: pedestrians, cyclists, transit riders and drivers sharing a right-of-way engineered for none exclusively. NACTO's *Urban Street Design Guide* is its drawing manual: lane diets, protected bike lanes, curb extensions, transit priority — the reallocation vocabulary the project's sections cite dimensionally ([[Street Transformation Sections]], [[Street-Space Reallocation]]).

Its usefulness to the brain is comparative: Complete Streets shows what reallocation achieves *within* a car-normal frame — every mode accommodated, the car retained as equal tenant. The European lineage the project follows goes further: filtered permeability and the woonerf make the car a *guest*, not a tenant ([[Filtered Permeability]], [[Woonerf]]).

The distinction sharpens the project's position for a jury: `<stadt.hub>` is not a complete-streets retrofit but a **network redesign** — the section drawings borrow NACTO's dimensions while the plan borrows Groningen's asymmetry ([[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]], [[Street as Public Space]]).
