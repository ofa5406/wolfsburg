# Desire Lines

The city's honest feedback channel. A **desire line** (desire path) is the worn trace where feet actually go — the diagonal through the lawn that overrules the right-angled pavement. Planners have read them for a century as *revealed preference in mud*: infrastructure proposed by use. The design folklore — some campuses paved only after watching where paths appeared — encodes the principle: **observe first, formalise second**.

The project applies the principle at three scales. At the hub: Zone 2's decision space and the directional paving are aligned to observed transfer movements, not compositional symmetry ([[Zone 2 — Threshold]], [[Directional Lines]]). In the network: the Rad algorithm is a desire-line engine in software — Dijkstra following least-resistance routes through the real street graph, flagging where the infrastructure contradicts the demand ([[Rad Network Algorithm]]). In the process: tactical pilots are desire-line experiments at street scale — let use draw the plan before concrete fixes it ([[Tactical Urbanism]], [[Minneapolis Mobility Hub Pilot]]).

The deeper stance: the user is a co-designer whose pencil is repetition ([[Smooth Usage]], [[Walkability]]).
