# Vision Zero

The ethics that reframed road death. **Vision Zero**, adopted by the Swedish parliament in 1997, states that **no loss of life is an acceptable price for mobility** — and, decisively, relocates responsibility: since humans predictably err, the *system designer*, not the erring road user, owns the fatal outcome. Design for the mistake: survivable speeds (30 km/h where people and vehicles mix), forgiving geometry, separation where speed is high.

Its results made it doctrine — Sweden's road deaths roughly halved as traffic grew, and cities worldwide adopted the frame; Oslo's centre reached **zero pedestrian and cyclist deaths in 2019**, parking removal included in the toolkit ([[Oslo Parking Removal]]); Pontevedra has had none since 2011 ([[Pontevedra]]).

The project inherits both the standard and the logic. The zones' internal speeds, raised crossings and filtered through-traffic are Vision Zero geometry ([[Traffic Calming]], [[Raised Narrowed Carriageway]], [[Hub-Integrated Crossing]]); and the system-designer ethic matches the brain's own stance — outcomes are design properties, not user failures ([[Human Scale]], [[Five-Zone Model]]).
