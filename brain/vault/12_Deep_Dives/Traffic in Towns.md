# Traffic in Towns

The report that made limits respectable (1963). Commissioned by the UK Ministry of Transport at motorisation's euphoric peak and led by Colin Buchanan, *Traffic in Towns* set out to accommodate the car scientifically — and concluded, with establishment authority, that **full accommodation destroys the town**: beyond a calculable ceiling, road capacity consumes the environmental quality it was meant to serve ([[Colin Buchanan]]).

Its enduring instruments: the concept of **environmental areas** — precincts where daily life outranks through-movement, fed by a distributor hierarchy — and the explicit framing of traffic as a *design quantity* to be chosen, not a demand to be met. Both anticipate the filtered city by a decade ([[Filtered Permeability]], [[Five-Zone Model]]).

Read today it is double-edged: its road-hierarchy thinking also rationalised much brutal rebuilding. The project cites it for the admission at its core — even the car's own great study found the ceiling — making the post-car turn a continuation of honest engineering, not its rejection ([[Car Dependency]], [[Post-Car City]]).
