# The High Cost of Free Parking

The project's single most load-bearing text (2005, updated 2011). Donald Shoup's 700-page treatise did for parking what earlier economics did for congestion: revealed a vast, unpriced externality hiding as normality. The pillars: parking is free to drivers for ~99% of US trips while its cost hides in rents and prices ([[Parking Free for 99 Percent of Trips]]); minimum parking requirements rest on circular pseudo-science and act as a >10× impact fee ([[Parking Minimums Circular Logic]]); the land and construction value of US parking plausibly exceeds the value of all cars ([[61 Square Metres per Parking Space]]).

Its constructive half — abolish minimums, price the kerb by demand, return revenue to the block — seeded the parking-reform movement now abolishing minimums across continents ([[Parking Reform]], [[Donald Shoup]]).

The book gives the Wolfsburg thesis its economic spine: the parking city is not a preference, it is a *pricing error* — and the hub is the corrected price made spatial ([[Parking City]], [[From Parking City to Hub City]]).
