# Marchetti Constant

The anthropological invariant under every mobility plan: people budget **about one hour a day for travel** — roughly half an hour each way — and have apparently done so since the Neolithic. Cesare Marchetti's 1994 paper (building on Yacov Zahavi's travel-time-budget work) showed the constant holding across centuries and cultures: what changes with technology is not time spent but **distance covered** — the city's radius is whatever thirty minutes buys.

Hence the shape of history: the walking city spans ~5 km; the tram city stretches along its lines; the car city balloons to a 30-km radius — sprawl is the constant, monetised ([[Car Dependency]]). Faster transport never saved time; it spent the savings on distance ([[Induced Demand]]).

The constant powers the proximity argument from below: if the budget is fixed, the design variable is **what a half-hour reaches** — and the 15-minute city is simply the Marchetti budget satisfied locally, at walking speed ([[15-Minute City]], [[Chrono-Urbanism]], [[Walking Catchment]]). Wolfsburg's hub grain re-prices the half-hour in destinations rather than kilometres ([[Proximity Planning]]).
