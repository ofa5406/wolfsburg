# History of Car Sharing

Older than the motorway network: organised car sharing begins in **Zurich, 1948**, with the **Sefage** cooperative (*Selbstfahrergemeinschaft*) — households sharing vehicles because individually they could not afford one. Scarcity invented the model; the postwar boom then buried it, as ownership became the norm the century built around ([[Car Dependency]]).

The revival came from environmentalism and pragmatism: Switzerland's Mobility cooperative and Germany's StattAuto (Berlin, 1988 — the pun: *statt Auto*, "instead of a car") professionalised station-based sharing; Bremen wove it into public policy and street infrastructure ([[Bremen mobil.punkt]], [[Station-Based Car-Sharing]]); the smartphone unlocked free-floating fleets in the 2010s ([[Free-Floating Car-Sharing]]); and the German industry consolidated its evidence base — the substitution ratios the project quotes ([[One Shared Car Replaces 11 Private Cars]], [[Bremen 16-to-1 Substitution]]).

The historical shape is the argument: sharing is not a novel behaviour awaiting adoption but the *original* relationship to the automobile, displaced by a marketing century and now returning with better logistics ([[Access over Ownership]], [[Shared Mobility]]).
