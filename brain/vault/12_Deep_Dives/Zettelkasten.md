# Zettelkasten

The method this brain is built on. The **Zettelkasten** ("slip box") is the note-per-idea, link-between-notes knowledge system perfected by sociologist **Niklas Luhmann** (1927–1998), whose wooden boxes held ~90,000 numbered slips, each carrying one thought and the addresses of its relatives. Luhmann credited the box as his co-author — the linking, he argued, made it a *communication partner*: following connections produced thoughts neither he nor any single slip contained.

The principles are visible in this vault: **atomicity** (one claim per note — a finding is separated from the concept it supports), **explicit linking** (typed edges rather than folders as the primary structure), and **emergence** (the graph view showing clusters and bridges no outline planned) ([[Project Brain]], [[Session Memory System]]).

Obsidian and its kin digitised the method; the 3D graph the exhibition shows is Luhmann's box rendered navigable ([[Summaery Exhibition]]). The wager is his too: that a project's intelligence lives less in its documents than in the **connections between them** — which is the literal thing the visitor explores.
