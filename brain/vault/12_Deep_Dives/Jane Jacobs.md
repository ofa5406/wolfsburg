# Jane Jacobs

The intellectual origin of the project's lineage. Jane Jacobs (1916–2006), journalist without planning credentials, published *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* in 1961 and dismantled the orthodoxy of car-centric urban renewal from direct observation of her own Greenwich Village: the street, not the road, is the city's primary public space and its engine of safety and vitality.

Her core instruments survive in the project's vocabulary: **eyes on the street** — informal surveillance from doors, windows and passers-by as the real infrastructure of urban safety ([[Eyes on the Street]]); fine-grained **mixed use** keeping pavements populated across the day ([[Mixed Use]]); and short blocks, density and old buildings as diversity's preconditions ([[Density]]). She also *practised* resistance — helping defeat Robert Moses' Lower Manhattan Expressway.

Sixty years later, hub design is applied Jacobs: the S-hub's bench, kiosk and lighting are eyes-on-the-street generators placed where parked cars used to blind the pavement ([[Public Life]], [[Street as Public Space]]).
