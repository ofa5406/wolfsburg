# Peak Car

The hypothesis that the car century has a summit. **Peak car** — argued from the 2010s by Phil Goodwin, David Metz and others — observes that per-capita car travel in most developed economies **stopped growing around the early 2000s** and has plateaued or declined since, especially in cities and among the young ([[Young Germans Drive Less]]). Candidate causes: saturation (a trip budget can only hold so much driving — [[Marchetti Constant]]), urbanisation, licensing decline, cost, digital substitution.

The honest scientific status is contested: sceptics read a pause shaped by income and fuel prices, not a turning; COVID and e-commerce scrambled the series. The brain stores it at medium confidence as a *tailwind, not a proof* ([[Millennials Still Want Cars]]).

Its use in the argument is about direction of history: the project is not reversing a rising tide but **steering a plateau** — infrastructure decisions made now choose which side of the peak Wolfsburg descends ([[Post-Car City]], [[Implementation Timeline]]). Even the most car-saturated city in Germany is planning on a curve that has stopped climbing ([[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]).
