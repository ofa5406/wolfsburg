# Right to the City

The philosophical charge under the land argument. Henri Lefebvre (*Le Droit à la ville*, 1968) argued the city is a collective **œuvre** — a work made by its inhabitants — and claimed for them a right not merely to reside but to *shape* urban space and share fully in urban life. David Harvey sharpened it: the right to the city is "the right to change ourselves by changing the city," and the question is always **who benefits from the production of urban space**.

The project's spatial-justice statistics are this theory quantified: cars carrying 13% of trips on 50% of street space is a distribution-of-the-œuvre question ([[Paris 13 Percent Trips 50 Percent Space]], [[Street as Public Space]]); the [[Car-Land Composite Map]] is a Lefebvrian audit of who was allocated the commons.

It also names the standard the transformation must itself meet: reclaimed land can gentrify as easily as liberate, which is why the equity table and the participation phases are structural rather than decorative ([[Equity of the Post-Car Transition]], [[Imposition Without Consent]]) — the right belongs to Gertrude and Lukas, not to the renderings ([[Public-Space Dividend]]).
