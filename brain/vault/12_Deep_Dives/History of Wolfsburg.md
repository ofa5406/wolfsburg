# History of Wolfsburg

A city younger than its residents' grandparents, founded for a car. On **1 July 1938** the National Socialist state established the "Stadt des KdF-Wagens bei Fallersleben" — the town of the Strength-through-Joy car — as the worker settlement for the new Volkswagen plant, on planned-city lines beside the Mittellandkanal ([[Mittellandkanal]]). The war diverted the plant to military production with forced labour — a history the company documents in its own remembrance work. In **May 1945** the town renamed itself **Wolfsburg**, after the Renaissance castle on its edge.

The postwar decades wrote the *Wirtschaftswunder* in one city: the Beetle poured out of the halls ([[Volkswagen Beetle]]), the population multiplied, and the town became the purest built expression of car-age planning — founded for the car, laid out for the car, paid by the car ([[Autogerechte Stadt]], [[Parking City]]).

That origin is the project's deepest premise: **Wolfsburg has already reinvented itself around a mobility technology once.** The city that was designed from zero for the private car is, of all cities, the one entitled to design itself past it ([[Post-Car City in VW's Hometown]], [[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]).
