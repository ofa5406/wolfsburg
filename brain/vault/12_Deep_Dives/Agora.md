# Agora

The oldest ancestor in the hub's family tree. The Greek **agora** — from *ageirein*, "to gather" — was the open assembly ground of the polis: simultaneously market, court, forum, sanctuary and promenade. Its genius was **superimposition**: commerce, politics and sociability shared one surface, so a citizen came for bread and stayed for the debate. The Roman forum formalised it; the medieval market square, the Ottoman square with its köşk ([[History of the Kiosk]]), and the European Platz carried it forward.

The car century broke the superimposition: movement and storage claimed the shared surface, and the gathering functions scattered into buildings ([[Parking City]], [[Street as Public Space]]).

The hub is the project's candidate for the agora's next form: a surface where transfer (the trip), exchange (kiosk, lockers, market slots) and assembly (seating, play, events) superimpose again — arriving somewhere being once more the same act as *being* somewhere ([[Hub as Place]], [[Pop-up Market Slot]], [[Public Life]]). The exhibition's taped parking space, furnished as a living room, restages the agora at 5×2.5 metres ([[Summaery Exhibition]]).
