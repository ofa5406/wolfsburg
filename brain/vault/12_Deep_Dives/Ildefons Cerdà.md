# Ildefons Cerdà

The engineer who invented the word. Ildefons Cerdà (1815–1876), Catalan civil engineer, designed Barcelona's 1859 Eixample — the chamfered-corner grid the superblocks now recompose — and in his *Teoría General de la Urbanización* (1867) **coined "urbanización"**: the first systematic theory that the city could be an object of scientific study and deliberate design ([[Superblock]]).

The historical irony the project inherits: Cerdà dimensioned his generous 113-metre blocks and wide streets for light, air and the *mobility of his time* — and the automobile century filled that generosity with traffic and parked cars. Rueda's superblocks are explicitly a *recovery* of Cerdà's intention from the car's occupation: the grid was never meant to be a machine for storage ([[Salvador Rueda]], [[Barcelona Superblocks]]).

For the brain, Cerdà anchors the longest arc in the graph: urbanism begins as an engineer's response to a mobility revolution (the railway and the industrial city), and each mobility revolution since has redrawn the discipline — the project being one more turn of that wheel ([[Etymology of Urban]], [[Post-Car City]]).
