# Jan Gehl

The methodologist of the lineage — the bridge from critique to measurement. Jan Gehl (b. 1936), Danish architect, spent five decades studying how people actually use public space, beginning with *Life Between Buildings* (1971) and codified in *Cities for People* (2010). His signature move: **count public life the way engineers count traffic** — pedestrian flows, "staying activities," sitting, lingering — so the quality of a street becomes evidence, not opinion.

His firm's before/after studies underpinned Copenhagen's decades-long pedestrianisation and consulted for Melbourne, New York and dozens of cities; he wrote the foreword to Moreno's 15-minute-city book, joining the lineage's ends ([[Carlos Moreno]]).

The project inherits Gehl twice. His **human scale** doctrine — design for 5 km/h perception, not 50 — is the hub palette's dimensional conscience ([[Human Scale]], [[Bench]]). And his measurement method is computable in the toolchain: the Activity Map's encounter-potential index is a Gehl survey run as an algorithm ([[Social Vitality Scoring]], [[Public Life]]).
