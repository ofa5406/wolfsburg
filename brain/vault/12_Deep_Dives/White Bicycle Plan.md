# White Bicycle Plan

Bike sharing's anarchist birth certificate. In **1965**, Amsterdam's Provo movement — Luud Schimmelpennink's *Witte Fietsenplan* — painted bicycles white and left them unlocked on the streets: free collective property against "the asphalt terror of the motorised bourgeoisie." The police confiscated the bikes (unlocked bicycles invited theft, they reasoned, with accidental irony); the plan survived as legend and prototype.

Every subsequent generation refined the same idea against its failure mode: La Rochelle's municipal yellow bikes (1974), Copenhagen's coin-deposit ByCyklen (1995), the smart-card and GPS systems of the 2000s (Vélib' 2007 at scale), the dockless flood of the 2010s — which promptly rediscovered the original problem as pavements drowned in abandoned bikes ([[Bike Sharing]], [[Micromobility]]).

The project reads the sixty-year experiment's verdict plainly: **shared micromobility works when it is anchored** — docked, maintained, legible — which is why the fleet's 131 e-bikes live in fixed racks at hubs rather than free-floating ([[E-bike Docking Rack]], [[Fleet Sizing Formula Accepted]]). Provo's politics, meanwhile, survive in the thesis itself: the street reclaimed from the car ([[Street-Space Reallocation]]).
