# Hans Monderman

The traffic engineer who removed the traffic engineering. Hans Monderman (1945–2008), Dutch, developed **shared space**: strip a street of signals, signs, markings and kerbs, and drivers — deprived of instructions — slow down and negotiate by eye contact. His Drachten intersection became the famous demonstration: signals removed, capacity maintained, injuries down. His axiom: *"When you treat people like idiots, they'll behave like idiots."*

Shared space is the radical cousin of the project's street philosophy. The woonerf domesticated the car with design rather than signage ([[History of the Woonerf]], [[Woonerf]]); Monderman generalised the psychology: **risk perception, not regulation, sets behaviour** — uncertainty makes drivers careful ([[Traffic Calming]]).

The hub palette applies him at every threshold: the [[Raised Narrowed Carriageway]] and [[Hub-Integrated Crossing]] work by material continuity and narrowing rather than signal control — the pedestrian surface flows through, the driver reads guest status from the ground itself ([[Zone 1 — Vehicle Edge]], [[Human Scale]]). His honest limit is also recorded: shared space serves confident negotiators best, so tactile guidance stays for those who navigate differently ([[Tactile Paving Strips]]).
