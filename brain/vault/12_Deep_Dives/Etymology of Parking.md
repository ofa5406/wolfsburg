# Etymology of Parking

The strangest etymology in the project: **parking comes from *park* — the green enclosure.** Medieval Latin *parricus* (fenced enclosure) gave Old French *parc*, a hunting preserve; the word meant landscape for six centuries. The military borrowed it first — an artillery *park* was the enclosure where guns and wagons stood — and 19th-century American usage extended that to vehicles standing on the roadside. By the 1920s *parking* meant leaving a car, and the *parking lot* completed the inversion.

So the word performs the exact theft the project describes: **a term for green public ground was captured, within one generation, by stored machinery** ([[Parking City]], [[Car-Dedicated Land]]). Every "parking lot" is etymologically a park with the trees deleted.

Which gives the transformation its neatest linguistic form: converting parking back to parks is not wordplay but restitution — the language remembers what the land was for ([[Reclaimed Land]], [[Public-Space Dividend]], [[From Parking City to Hub City]]).
