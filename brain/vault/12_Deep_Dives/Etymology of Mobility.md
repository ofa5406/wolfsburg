# Etymology of Mobility

**Mobility** descends from Latin *mobilis* — "movable, easy to move" — a contraction of *movibilis*, from *movēre*, to move. Classical Latin already used it socially: *mobilitas* meant both physical movability and *fickleness of mind* — and the *mobile vulgus*, the "movable crowd," gave English the word **mob** in the 1680s. Mobility has always named people in motion, with a ruling-class flicker of anxiety about it.

Two modern senses split in the 19th–20th centuries: **social mobility** (movement between classes) and **transport mobility** (movement through space) — and the project's central claim is that they never really separated: access to movement *is* access to opportunity, which is why the equity table is a mobility document ([[Equity of the Post-Car Transition]], [[Gertrude]]).

"Mobility as a Service" completes the arc: the word that began as a *property of things* (movability) becomes a *provision* — something a city can supply, guarantee and design for, rather than something each household must own privately ([[Mobility as a Service]], [[Access over Ownership]]).
