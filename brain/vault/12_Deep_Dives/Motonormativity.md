# Motonormativity

The cognitive bias the whole debate swims in. **Motonormativity** — coined by psychologist Ian Walker (Walker, Tapp & Davis, 2023) — names society's unconscious double standard for cars: harms tolerated from driving that would be scandalous from any other source. The study's method was elegant: survey questions with "car" swapped for other risks — respondents who rejected "people shouldn't smoke in populated areas where others breathe the fumes" accepted the identical sentence about driving. The bias held **even among non-drivers**: it is cultural water, not personal interest.

The concept explains the asymmetries the project keeps meeting: why 61 m² of car storage reads as normal but a bench "wastes space" ([[61 Square Metres per Parking Space]]); why road deaths are weather but zone filters are tyranny ([[Imposition Without Consent]]); why the car-free proposal, not the status quo, carries the burden of proof ([[Car Dependency]], [[German Car Culture]]).

Its strategic use is diagnostic, never accusatory: the boards surface the double standard with Walker's swap trick — let the audience catch the bias in their own answer ([[Behaviour Change]]).
