# MaaS Origins in Finland

Mobility as a Service has a birthplace and almost a birthday. The concept crystallised in Helsinki around 2014: **Sonja Heikkilä's** Aalto University master's thesis proposed mobility as a subscribed service integrating all modes in one interface, while **Sampo Hietanen** — then leading ITS Finland, later founding MaaS Global — evangelised the framing of "the Netflix of transportation." Finland's transport ministry adopted the agenda, and the 2018 Act on Transport Services made the decisive move: **operators were legally required to open their ticketing and data interfaces** ([[MaaS Is Governance-Hard]]).

That law is why Whim launched in Helsinki and not elsewhere ([[Whim Helsinki]]): the app was downstream of the legislation. The subsequent decade wrote both halves of the lesson — measurable behavioural effect ([[Whim Year One Numbers]]), fragile business models (MaaS Global's financial trouble).

For the project, the Finnish origin fixes MaaS's true genus: **it is transport policy wearing a software interface** ([[Mobility as a Service]], [[Integrated Ticketing]]) — and Wolfsburg's version budgets for the legislation, not just the app ([[MaaS Levels]]).
