# Risk — Car Culture Resistance

The behavioural objection: *"Germans love their cars — nobody will give them up."* The resistance is documented — the "car state" culture of automotive regions, acceptance collapsing when measures read as restricting personal freedom, Berlin's stalled car-reduction politics ([[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]], [[German Car Culture]]).

The counter-evidence strip is national and recent: the **Deutschlandticket** moved German modal share measurably in one year (+16% transit, +30% rail, −6.7 Mt CO₂) ([[Deutschlandticket Modal Shift]]); Frankfurt acceptability research finds "surprisingly high" support for car-reduction when local benefits are tangible, with push measures the most effective lever ([[Push Beats Pull Alone]]); Groningen holds ~60% cycling half a century after its plan ([[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]]); and the youth ownership decline runs underneath it all ([[Young Germans Drive Less]]).

The design consequence: pair every pull with an honest push — the research says push is what actually moves people, so the project does not hide the parking removal behind the shiny hubs ([[Parking Relocation Effect]], [[Push and Pull Measures]]). And never fight identity frontally: the culture is offered a succession, not a surrender ([[Identity Labeling Nudge]]).
