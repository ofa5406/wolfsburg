# Risk — Tech Saviour Critique

The framing risk the midterm brief explicitly warned against: *"Strip away the renders and this is a smartphone app that dispatches pods — a technology product answering a spatial and political question."* The academic critique of smart-city solutionism is well developed, and a jury primed by it will read an app-centric pitch as shallow.

The counter is that the load-bearing move of the thesis **is spatial, not digital**: the organising element shifts from parking to hubs, and freed car-land becomes public life — the reallocation of roughly half the street surface to people ([[From Parking City to Hub City]], [[Paris 13 Percent Trips 50 Percent Space]]). The precedents achieved their revolutions with paint, bollards and circulation plans, not apps ([[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]], [[Ghent Circulation Plan]]).

The discipline it imposes on every presentation: lead with the land; the hero image is a *place*, never a phone; the dispatch layer enters as plumbing ([[Hub as Place]]). The prepared slide — *"What stays if the app dies?"* — answers itself: the hubs, the filtered streets, the frequencies and the public space are all physical or scheduled ([[Catalyst Not Shelter]]).
