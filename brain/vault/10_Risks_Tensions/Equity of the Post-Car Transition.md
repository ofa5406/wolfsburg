# Equity of the Post-Car Transition

The fairness question, treated as a design brief rather than a disclaimer: *"Does this serve the elderly, disabled, families and night-shift workers — or only fit young app-users?"* The research is blunt: shared mobility is routinely designed without disabled access in mind, apps exclude the digitally excluded, on-demand pricing beats scheduled transit's affordability, and late-shift transit commutes run about twice the car's time ([[Shift Workers Face Double Commutes]]).

The project's answer is a **stated design standard**, not a paragraph of intent: maximum walk-to-hub distance as a guaranteed metric ([[Hub Coverage Map]]); a fixed share of wheelchair-accessible vehicles (the ID. Buzz's flat-floor van form helps); booking by phone and at-hub terminal, never app-only ([[Real-time Info Terminal]]); **guaranteed 24/7 service on the factory corridor** for night shifts; capped fares so the system is never pricier than transit ([[Integrated Ticketing]]).

The strategic point the register insists on: equity is the political shield. Restrictions target where alternatives are strong; generosity goes where car dependence is structural — so the transformation never hands its opponents the "war on the worker" story ([[Imposition Without Consent]], [[Anna]]).
