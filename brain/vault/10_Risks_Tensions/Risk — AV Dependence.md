# Risk — AV Dependence

The most defensible attack on the project: *"You allocate 369 autonomous micro-pods and 33 autonomous buses. Driverless shuttles have a graveyard of cancelled pilots. What happens if they never arrive?"* The graveyard is real: Navya insolvent (2025), Europe's flagship CAVForth autonomous bus route ended unrenewed, pilots in Toronto, San Francisco and Virginia folded with their suppliers, and both Cruise and Waymo have issued four-figure recalls ([[Low-Speed Shuttle Class]], [[Cruise Shutdown]]).

The counter is phasing with a non-AV fallback: **every vehicle class in the fleet has a same-day human-driven equivalent that already operates commercially** — pooled vans à la MOIA Hamburg, shuttle buses with drivers, e-bikes, shared EVs. The city works on day one with drivers; autonomy lowers operating cost over time but is *not load-bearing for the urban argument* ([[Phased Autonomy]], [[Implementation Timeline]]). Detroit's Connect shuttle shows the pattern: launched driver-supervised, upgrading to autonomous.

The prepared slide: *"What if AVs are ten years late?"* — the network still functions; only the per-trip cost curve shifts ([[BerlKönig]], [[AV Reality Check]]).
