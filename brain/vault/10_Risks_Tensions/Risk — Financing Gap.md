# Risk — Financing Gap

The standard jury kill-shot: *"763 vehicles, 68 hubs, six car-park conversions — who pays, and what is the operating model?"* The risk is genuine beyond rhetoric: mobility-hub economics are hard, and providers often refuse to operate unless the public sector carries the financial risk.

The answer is the three-layer [[Funding Stack]], built from running systems rather than hope: cities fund sites and shells (Berlin Jelbi and Vancouver Mobi PPPs), operators finance their own fleets — with MOIA's turnkey licensing removing the city's fleet-capex risk entirely ([[MOIA]]) — and land-value capture closes the loop on the Hong Kong MTR rail-plus-property model, applicable precisely because the hubs sit on appreciating freed land ([[Surface Lot to Dozens of Homes]], [[L-Anchor]]).

The transitional revenue instrument has a German-adjacent precedent: Nottingham's workplace parking levy visibly funding the tram ([[Nottingham Workplace Parking Levy]]). The honest tension — operator risk appetite — stays named on the slide, answered by the value-capture mechanism rather than waved away ([[Risk Register]], [[Implementation Timeline]]).
