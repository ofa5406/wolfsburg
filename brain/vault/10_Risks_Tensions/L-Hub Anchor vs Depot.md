# L-Hub Anchor vs Depot

The tension at the centre of the June 17 debate, and the cleanest example of the project resolving a contradiction by **splitting the concept**. One reading of the L-hub made it a *civic anchor* — the converted car park as neighbourhood centre with gastronomy, community space and housing. The other made it a *fleet depot* — charging, maintenance, storage for 763 vehicles ([[EV Fleet Charging at Scale]], [[Fleet Maintenance Station]]). One building struggling to be both risks being neither: a depot with a café, or an anchor whose courtyard is a service yard.

The resolution assigns each role a geography ([[Hub System Rethink]]): central conversions become **L-Anchors** — mostly non-parking public programme, incidental servicing at most ([[L-Anchor]]) — while edge car parks become **L-Gateways** — depot, maintenance and park-and-switch interchange where inbound drivers leave the private car ([[L-Gateway]], [[Park-and-Switch Interchange]]).

Residual open questions are tracked, not hidden: whether Gateways count inside the "6 L-hubs" total or as a separate infrastructure layer, and the tool's inability to place central L-Anchors ([[Intermodal Hub Algorithm]], [[Coverage Radius Mismatch]]).
