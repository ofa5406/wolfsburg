# Freight and Emergency Access

The operational objection every filtered-permeability scheme faces: *"Your zones block through-traffic — so deliveries can't reach businesses, ambulances get delayed, and traffic piles onto the boundary roads."* Emotionally potent, and legitimate enough to require a drawn answer.

The evidence is unusually clean: across 46 London LTNs, internal traffic fell ~47% with **no corresponding boundary-road rise**, and emergency services — consulted before implementation — typically report **no change in response times** ([[LTN 46 Percent Internal Traffic Drop]]). The mechanism: only *through*-traffic is filtered; residents, freight, refuse and emergency vehicles retain full access, increasingly via ANPR cameras and retractable bollards rather than fixed barriers ([[Modal Filter]], [[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]]).

The deliverable this risk generates: a **"who can still drive" layer** on the zone map — access traffic, time-windowed deliveries, hub-based micro-logistics with cargo bikes for the last metres ([[Parcel Pickup Lockers]], [[E-Cargo Bike]]), emergency overrides — so the answer is cartographic, not verbal ([[Five-Zone Model]], [[Freight and Emergency Access]] is closed by drawing, not debating).
