# Wolfsburg

The site, the subject and the test. Wolfsburg, Lower Saxony: **founded in 1938 for the sole purpose of building cars** — the planned company town of the Volkswagen works — today ~130,000 residents, Germany's richest city by GDP per capita (~€120,000) and its most car-saturated: 956 cars per 1,000 residents ([[Richest City Built by the Car]], [[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]).

The city's form is its history legible: the plant and its 1.6 km² of halls on the north bank of the Mittellandkanal ([[Worlds Largest Car Factory]], [[Mittellandkanal]]), the centre laid out to serve it across the water, districts dimensioned around car movement and car storage, and Autostadt — the company's brand-world — as the city's front door ([[Autostadt]]).

For the project, Wolfsburg is the maximal case in both directions: nowhere is the parking city purer, and nowhere are the assets for its transformation more concentrated — one anchor institution, one predictable demand wave, and the manufacturer of the replacement system headquartered on site ([[Wolfsburg as Testbed]], [[Post-Car City in VW's Hometown]]). The study area covers the central districts ([[Wolfsburg Districts]]).
