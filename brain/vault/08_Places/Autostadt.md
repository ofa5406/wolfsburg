# Autostadt

Volkswagen's brand-world beside the plant: a landscaped campus of pavilions, museum, customer-delivery towers and event spaces opened in 2000, where buyers once collected new cars from glass silos — **the car as destination, celebrated at the scale of a theme park** ([[Volkswagen]], [[German Car Culture]]).

For the project, Autostadt matters as symbol and as audience. Symbolically it is the city's existing monument to automobility — the place where the twentieth-century bargain is staged daily — and thus the measure of what the project proposes to succeed: `<stadt.hub>` positions the mobility hub as the *next* thing this city builds monuments to ([[Post-Car City in VW's Hometown]], [[From Parking City to Hub City]]).

Practically, it anchors the visitor block of the demand basis — a major generator of the ~17,000 daily visitors, clustered near the station and the canal ([[Demand Basis]], [[Mittellandkanal]]) — which is why the L-tier presence around the station-Autostadt-plant triangle carries the tourist load as well as the commuter one ([[L-Hub]]).
