# Public Life

The activity between buildings — walking, lingering, meeting, playing, watching — that [[Jan Gehl]] made observable and countable. Gehl's method distinguishes necessary activities (which happen regardless of quality) from optional and social activities (which only happen when space invites them); the presence of optional activity is therefore a *measurement* of urban quality, not an opinion about it.

The project uses public life as its success metric. The goal of reclaiming car land "is not prettier traffic corridors — it is to ask what fundamentally new urban life becomes possible." Hubs are designed as public-life anchors first and interchanges second: the [[Ground Field]], seating, play and market elements exist to generate optional and social activity, and a before/after Gehl-style count is how the transformation would be evaluated in practice.
