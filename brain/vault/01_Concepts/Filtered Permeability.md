# Filtered Permeability

The network principle behind the project's 5-zone structure: a neighbourhood should be **fully permeable for walking and cycling but deliberately not permeable for through motor traffic**. The street grid stays fine and connected for people on foot and bike; for cars, selected links are filtered so the area cannot be used as a cut-through. The term was coined by [[Steve Melia]] in 2012.

The physical device is the [[Modal Filter]] — bollards, planters, bus gates, or at Wolfsburg's scale, restricted zone entrances. The applied urban form is the [[Low-Traffic Neighbourhood]], and the city-sector-scale proof cases are [[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]] (1977) and [[Ghent Circulation Plan]] (2017). In the project it is implemented as the [[Five-Zone Model]]: one-way internal streets, restricted entrances, priority for shared mobility and active travel, direct access maintained for residents and services.
