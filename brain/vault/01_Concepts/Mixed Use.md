# Mixed Use

The interleaving of housing, work, retail, culture and services at fine grain, as opposed to the single-use zoning that separates life into drive-connected pods. Mixed use shortens trips (the corner shop replaces the retail-park run), spreads activity across the day (offices by day, restaurants by evening), and supplies the foot traffic that [[Eyes on the Street]] and street commerce both need.

It is a precondition for both of the project's demand-side pillars: [[Proximity Planning]] fails without nearby destinations to be proximate *to*, and [[Chrono-Urbanism]]'s multi-clock city needs programmes that alternate through the day. The L-hub strategy applies mixed use directly to mobility infrastructure: an [[L-Anchor]] stacks gastronomy, shops, community space and housing onto a repurposed car park, turning a mono-functional storage building into a mixed-use anchor.
