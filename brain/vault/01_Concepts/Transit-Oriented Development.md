# Transit-Oriented Development

The planning model that concentrates density and mixed use within walking distance of high-quality transit stops, so that location itself substitutes for car ownership. Codified by ITDP's *TOD Standard*, it links eight principles — walk, cycle, connect, transit, mix, densify, compact, shift — into a scoring system used worldwide.

The hub network is TOD's post-car descendant. Where classic TOD organises development around a rail station, the [[Hub System]] organises it around shared-mobility nodes, extending the logic to neighbourhoods that will never justify rail. The tier structure mirrors TOD's density gradient: the [[L-Anchor]] carries station-scale programme and housing, the [[M-Hub]] plays the role of a tram-stop centre, and the [[S-Hub]] extends coverage into the capillaries. The "shift" principle — reallocating space from cars — is the shared DNA.
