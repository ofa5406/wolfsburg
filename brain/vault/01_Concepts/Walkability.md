# Walkability

The degree to which the built environment makes walking safe, direct, comfortable and interesting. Its measurable ingredients are network connectivity (small blocks, no severance), destination density, footpath quality, shade and shelter, and perceived safety — [[Jane Jacobs]]'s "eyes on the street". Its enemies are wide fast carriageways, blank frontages, and parking deserts.

Walkability is the quiet load-bearing wall of the whole hub concept: every hub catchment is a **walking** catchment, so the system only works if the walk to the hub is acceptable. That is why the tier radii are set at 150–250 m for [[S-Hub]], 300–500 m for [[M-Hub]] and 700–1000 m for [[L-Hub]] — distances calibrated to what people will actually walk for different service levels — and why the [[Street Transformation Sections]] spend reclaimed land first on footpaths, crossings and trees.
