# Street as Public Space

The foundational reframing of post-car urbanism: a street is not a traffic pipe with buildings attached, but the city's primary public space that happens to carry movement. [[Jane Jacobs]] made the case in 1961 — the sidewalk ballet, "eyes on the street", fine-grained mixed use — and [[Jan Gehl]] spent decades building the observational methods that let designers measure life between buildings rather than just count vehicles.

The reframing matters operationally, not just rhetorically. If the street is public space, then car storage on it is a private occupation of common land, and reallocating it is restitution rather than loss. Every hub in the project extends this logic: the [[Ground Field]] claims a piece of street as unmistakably public surface, and the three-zone sequence choreographs the transition from vehicle to dwelling.
