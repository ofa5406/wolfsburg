# Hub City

The proposal. A city where the organising element of mobility is no longer the parking space but the [[Mobility Hub]]: a tiered network of 68 shared-mobility nodes (6 L, 19 M, 43 S) within a short walk of nearly every resident. Instead of every household storing a private car, a pooled fleet of autonomous shuttles, micro-pods, shared EVs and e-cargo bikes is accessed at hubs, while walking, cycling and public transport carry the everyday load.

The spatial consequence is the point: when storage is concentrated and shared, the in-between streets are freed. The Hub City reclaims dispersed parking land for housing, greenery, and public life — the [[Public-Space Dividend]]. The concept is the project's answer to the [[Parking City]] and the destination of the whole argument chain in [[From Parking City to Hub City]].
