# Chrono-Urbanism

The theoretical core beneath the [[15-Minute City]]: organising the city around **time and proximity rather than speed and distance**. Where twentieth-century planning optimised for velocity — how far can you get in thirty minutes — chrono-urbanism asks how much of life fits within a short, unhurried radius, and how buildings and spaces can serve multiple uses across the hours of the day.

The shift inverts the value of infrastructure. A highway is impressive under a speed metric and destructive under a time-of-life metric; a mixed-use street corner is invisible to traffic engineering and central to chrono-urbanism. For the Wolfsburg project the relevant application is the hub as a time-layered place: commuter interchange at shift change, market and play space at midday, lit social anchor in the evening — one surface, many clocks.
