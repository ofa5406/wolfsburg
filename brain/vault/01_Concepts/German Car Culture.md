# German Car Culture

The deep cultural attachment binding German identity, industry and the automobile: engineering pride, the Autobahn myth, the company car as status grammar, and an auto industry that employs, directly and indirectly, millions. Any post-car proposal in Germany argues against this current — and in Wolfsburg, the current's very source.

The project treats the culture as a design constraint, not an enemy. Three honest counter-observations ground the strategy: young urban Germans increasingly delay or skip car ownership; German cities (Bremen, Freiburg's Vauban, Berlin's Kiezblocks) already host celebrated car-light neighbourhoods; and VW itself runs Europe's flagship ridepooling service ([[MOIA]]). The proposal is framed as the next chapter of Wolfsburg's mobility pride — the city that built the people's car now builds the people's mobility system — rather than a repudiation of it. The unresolved tension is logged as [[Post-Car City in VW's Hometown]].
