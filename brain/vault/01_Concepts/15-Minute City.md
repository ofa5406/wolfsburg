# 15-Minute City

[[Carlos Moreno]]'s 2016 formulation of proximity planning: neighbourhoods where residents reach most daily needs — work, shops, healthcare, education, leisure — within a fifteen-minute walk or bike ride. Moreno frames it through four principles: **proximity, diversity (mixed use), density, and ubiquity (access for all)**, organised by the deeper idea of [[Chrono-Urbanism]] — designing around time rather than distance.

In the project's architecture of arguments, the 15-minute city is the **demand side**: hubs and filtered permeability remove the need to drive, while proximity planning ensures destinations are already nearby. The hub is where the four principles become physical. The concept's flagship application is [[Paris 15-Minute City]] under Anne Hidalgo. The project also notes the conspiracy-theory backlash framing it as "movement restriction" and rebuts it plainly: the concept is about access, not confinement.
