# Traffic Evaporation

The empirical finding that when road capacity for cars is removed, a substantial share of the traffic does not divert — it disappears. Drivers change modes, retime trips, consolidate errands, or stop making marginal journeys. The canonical study is *Disappearing Traffic? The Story So Far* (Cairns, Atkins & Goodwin, 2002), which reviewed roughly 70 capacity-reduction cases and found average traffic reductions well beyond what diversion could explain ([[Disappearing Traffic Study]]).

Traffic evaporation is the mirror image of [[Induced Demand]]: capacity creates traffic, and its removal un-creates it. For the project it answers the most predictable objection to the [[Five-Zone Model]] — "the cars will just clog the boundary roads" — with a documented tendency: some will, most won't, and the effect fades as behaviour adapts.
