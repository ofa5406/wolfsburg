# Access over Ownership

The cultural and economic principle underneath shared mobility: what people need is not a two-tonne possession parked 95% of the time, but reliable **access** to movement when they want it. When access is pooled — through car sharing, ridepooling, bike share, transit — one vehicle serves many households, and the storage burden collapses.

The Bremen evidence makes the principle concrete: each shared car replaces roughly sixteen private ones ([[Bremen 16-to-1 Substitution]]). At city scale that arithmetic is what converts the [[Parking City]]'s storage land into the [[Hub City]]'s public space. The principle also reframes the hub itself: a [[Mobility Hub]] is the physical interface of access, the place where the pooled system becomes as tangible and trustworthy as a private car in the driveway used to be.
