# Induced Demand

The well-documented phenomenon that adding road capacity generates new car traffic rather than relieving congestion: faster roads make longer trips rational, attract development outward, and shift travellers from other modes until congestion returns to its previous level. It is the reason sixty years of road building never "solved" traffic, and it converts every highway-widening argument into a treadmill.

For a post-car project the concept cuts both ways. It condemns the supply-side instinct (build more lanes, build more parking — [[Parking Minimums]] are induced demand codified into zoning), and it underwrites the reverse move: if capacity creates traffic, removing capacity can dissolve it, which is what the [[Traffic Evaporation]] literature observes in practice. Together the pair forms the network-theory backbone of the project's traffic argument.
