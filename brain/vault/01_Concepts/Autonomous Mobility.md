# Autonomous Mobility

Passenger transport by vehicles that drive themselves — in this project always **shared and pooled**, never privately owned robotaxis replicating the parking problem with extra electronics. Automation matters to the thesis for one structural reason: it removes the driver-cost floor that makes small-vehicle, high-frequency, demand-responsive service uneconomic, allowing a fleet of 369 micro-pods and 55 shuttle pods to be dispatched like a utility.

The project is deliberately sober about readiness. As of mid-2026, driverless operation exists in bounded deployments (Waymo's US cities, pilot shuttles in Europe) but nowhere at whole-city scale — a fact logged in the risk register as [[AV Reality Check]], and answered by the [[Implementation Timeline]]: early phases run with drivers and remote supervision, automation arrives as it matures, and the *spatial* system (hubs, zones, reclaimed land) is valuable even if full autonomy is late. Automation levels are classified in [[Vehicle Automation Levels]].
