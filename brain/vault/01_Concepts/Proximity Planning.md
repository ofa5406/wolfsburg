# Proximity Planning

The umbrella term for planning approaches that bring daily destinations close to residents instead of bringing residents at speed to distant destinations. Its ancestors are [[Jane Jacobs]]'s fine-grained mixed use and the neighbourhood-unit tradition; its contemporary champion is [[Carlos Moreno]] with the [[15-Minute City]]; its practice arms are mixed-use zoning, ground-floor programming, and the co-location of services.

Proximity is the demand-side half of any post-car strategy. Supply-side moves — hubs, filters, reallocation — make driving less necessary and less convenient, but only proximity makes *not driving* genuinely pleasant. In the Wolfsburg plan, proximity is delivered through the hub network itself: each [[M-Hub]] doubles as a neighbourhood service point, and each [[L-Hub]] anchors gastronomy, shops and community programme, so that the mobility node and the daily-needs node are the same place.
