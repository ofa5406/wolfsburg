# Parking Reform

The policy movement that undoes parking's hidden subsidies: abolishing [[Parking Minimums]], pricing curb space at demand-responsive rates, capping or taxing structured parking, and converting existing parking assets to other uses. Its intellectual founder is [[Donald Shoup]]; its measurable claim is that parking in the US is free for ~99% of car trips while its total land and construction value exceeds the value of all cars ([[Parking Free for 99 Percent of Trips]]).

For the project, parking reform is the *policy shadow* of a spatial proposal. The hub network is, in Shoupian terms, parking demand collectivised and priced rather than mandated and free. The L-hub strategy of converting multi-storey garages ([[L-Hub]]) is parking reform executed as architecture: the same asset, recycled into shared-mobility infrastructure and public programme.
