# Tactical Urbanism

Short-term, low-cost, reversible interventions — paint, planters, temporary furniture, pilot closures — used to test permanent change before committing to it. Its power is political as much as spatial: a trial defuses the fear of irreversibility, generates real usage data, and lets residents experience a street they could not have imagined from a drawing.

In the project's four-phase [[Implementation Timeline]], tactical moves carry the first phase: pilot modal filters, a first pop-up hub, temporary reclamation of one symbolic parking lot. Ghent's circulation plan — introduced literally overnight with signs and planters — proves the method scales to a whole inner city ([[Ghent Circulation Plan]]). Tactical urbanism is also the honest answer to the "what if it fails?" objection: if it fails, it is removed, and the city has lost only paint.
