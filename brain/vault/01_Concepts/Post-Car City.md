# Post-Car City

A post-car city is not a city with no cars. It is a city where the private car is no longer the **organising assumption** of urban form — where streets, land allocation and daily life are designed around walking, cycling, transit and shared mobility, and the private car is demoted to an occasional, edge-of-system option.

The idea sits in a sixty-year lineage: [[Jane Jacobs]] attacked the erosion of street life by car-centric planning; [[Colin Buchanan]] conceded that a city cannot give the car everything it wants; [[Jan Gehl]] built the methods for measuring human-scale alternatives; [[Donald Shoup]] diagnosed parking as the hidden mechanism; [[Carlos Moreno]] operationalised proximity. The project positions itself as the next step in that chain: making the [[Mobility Hub]], not the parking space, the unit that organises the neighbourhood.
