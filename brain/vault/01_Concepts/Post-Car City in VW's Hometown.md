# Post-Car City in VW's Hometown

The project's symbolic thesis, stated as a place: **there is no more meaningful location on Earth to design a post-private-car city than the hometown of Volkswagen.** Wolfsburg was founded in 1938 to build cars; it is Germany's richest city by GDP per capita and its most car-saturated ([[Richest City Built by the Car]], [[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]). If the car's own capital can reorganise itself around shared mobility, the argument is settled everywhere the conditions are milder.

The symbolism cuts in both directions, and the narrative is engineered for the dangerous one. Read as attack — outsiders designing away the town's product amid mass job cuts — it is politically dead on arrival ([[Wolfsburg Privileged Identity Study]], [[VW Cuts 35000 Jobs]]). Read as succession — the town demonstrating its company's own declared next business — it recruits the identity instead of fighting it ([[NEW AUTO Strategy]], [[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]).

Every deliverable carries a trace of this frame: the factory-gate case study, the shift-wave proof, the L-hub conversions of the parking city's own monuments ([[From Parking City to Hub City]], [[VW Factory Gate]]).
