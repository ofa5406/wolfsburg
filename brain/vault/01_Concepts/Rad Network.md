# Rad Network

The project's cycling spine — *Rad* is German for bike — and the quiet workhorse of the modal shift. The network is a continuous system of protected cycle routes threading the five zones, fully permeable exactly where the car network is filtered: the Groningen rule, applied as drawn geometry ([[Filtered Permeability]], [[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]]).

Design logic: a trunk-and-branch structure on the Houten pattern ([[Houten]]) — direct spines toward the centre and the plant, fine branches into the residential grain — with protected lanes taking over reclaimed kerb space in the first, cheapest phase of street transformation ([[On-Street Parking 20 Percent of Kerb Space]], [[Street Transformation Sections]]).

The evidence for prioritising it is the fastest-acting in the whole evidence bank: Paris's protected network roughly doubled cycling's modal share in five years; Ghent's rose ~60% alongside its circulation plan ([[Paris 15-Minute City]], [[Ghent Circulation Plan]]). The network is mapped and analysed as its own mode in the Activity Map ([[Wolfsburg Activity Map]]), and every hub docks into it — the e-bike fleet is useless without the lanes ([[E-Cargo Bike]], [[Active Travel]]).
