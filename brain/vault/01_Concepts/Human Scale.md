# Human Scale

Design calibrated to the human body and senses: the 5 km/h architecture of the pedestrian rather than the 60 km/h architecture of the windshield. [[Jan Gehl]]'s central observation is that the two produce incompatible cities — detail, doorways, and short distances reward the walker, while the driver needs simplification, setbacks and signage, so a city optimised for driving is experientially empty on foot.

The hub palette applies human scale deliberately. Canopy heights, bench spacing, planting, tactile paving and lighting are all specified at body scale in the [[Hub Toolpalette]]; the three-zone arrival sequence slows a traveller stepping off a pod from vehicle speed to dwelling speed within a few metres. Human scale is also the argument for many small hubs over few large ones: 43 [[S-Hub]] moments woven into streets, rather than terminals.
