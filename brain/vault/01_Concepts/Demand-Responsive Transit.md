# Demand-Responsive Transit

Transit without a fixed timetable: passengers request trips, and an algorithm continuously composes routes, pooling compatible requests into shared vehicles. DRT fills the service gap between the big bus (efficient only on thick corridors) and the private car (door-to-door but space-hungry), and it is the operating model for the project's shuttle pods and micro-pods.

The operational science is real and unforgiving: dispatch and routing quality decides whether a DRT fleet feels like magic or like a slow taxi. Key variables are detour ratio (how much pooling lengthens each trip), waiting time, and rebalancing (repositioning empty vehicles toward coming demand — [[Fleet Rebalancing]]). VW's own MOIA in Hamburg is the neighbouring proof that large-scale ridepooling works operationally ([[MOIA]]), and its planning tool MIA supplies the parameter vocabulary the project borrows ([[MIA Mobility Impact Analyzer]]).
