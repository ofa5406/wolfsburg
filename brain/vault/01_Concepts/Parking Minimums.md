# Parking Minimums

Zoning rules that require every new building to provide a fixed number of parking spaces — N per dwelling, N per 1,000 square feet of retail. [[Donald Shoup]]'s *The High Cost of Free Parking* (2005) showed these minimums are the invisible code of the car-dependent city: they force every building to bundle expensive car storage into its cost, raise rents, suppress density, consume land that could be housing, and subsidise driving into a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Shoup's three reforms — remove minimums, price curb parking by demand, return the revenue to the neighbourhood — define modern [[Parking Reform]]. In Germany the equivalent instrument is the Land-level *Stellplatzverordnung*, which several cities have relaxed or abolished; the project cites the US dollar figures as illustrations of the mechanism, not as German cost data.
