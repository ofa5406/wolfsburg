# Traffic Calming

The toolkit that reduces vehicle speeds and volumes through physical design rather than signage: raised tables, narrowed carriageways, chicanes, material changes, and tightened corner radii. Where filtered permeability edits the network's topology, traffic calming edits its geometry — the two together produce streets where low speed is self-enforcing.

Its intellectual origin is the Dutch [[Woonerf]], which abolished the pedestrian/carriageway split altogether. In the project, calming is built into the hub elements themselves: the [[Raised Narrowed Carriageway]] lifts the road surface to footpath level through the hub zone, the [[Hub-Integrated Crossing]] extends the ground field across the street, and the stone [[Ground Field]] signals by material that the car is a guest. Every hub thus doubles as a calming device — 68 slow points woven into the network.
