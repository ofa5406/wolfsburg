# Social Vitality Scoring

The Activity Map's social diagnosis (the "Greenery Analysis" mode): each district scored on four axes — **Green Coverage** (share of district area under parks and forest, computed by Sutherland–Hodgman polygon clipping for exact overlaps), **Social Density** (amenities weighted by community value: playgrounds 5, sports pitches 4, cafés 3, benches 1, divided by area), **Green Accessibility** (quality green *reachable from* the district: 3 points inside, 1 point within a short walk outside), and the composite **Encounter Potential Index** — 35% green coverage, 30% social density, 20% transit access, 15% pedestrian-path density ([[Wolfsburg Districts]]).

The index predicts where people are likely to bump into each other outdoors — [[Public Life]] as a computable quantity, in the lineage of Gehl's public-life measurement ([[Jan Gehl]]).

Its design output is a targeting map: low-encounter districts are where converting parking into benches, play and pocket parks buys the most social return — the [[Public-Space Dividend]] allocated by evidence rather than intuition ([[Reclaimed Land]], [[Eyes on the Street]]).
