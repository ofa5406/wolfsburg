# Greenery Mode

The Activity Map's natural-space inventory: every green and blue feature inside the city boundary, fetched live from OpenStreetMap and classified into eight toggleable categories — parks, grass, forests, agriculture, vegetation, individual trees, protected areas, and the water network of rivers and canals ([[OpenStreetMap Data]]).

Alone it is an inventory; combined with the district overlay and the scoring layer it becomes an argument ([[Social Vitality Scoring]]). Districts showing dense housing and thin green on this map are the candidates where reclaimed car space converts to pocket parks with the highest return — the "what does freed parking become?" question answered spatially, not rhetorically ([[Reclaimed Land]], [[Public-Space Dividend]]).

The water-network category earns its place for one proposal in particular: the canal-side parking-to-park conversion needs exactly this layer to draw its before/after ([[Mittellandkanal]]). And the eight-category structure quietly serves the hub palette too — bioswales, meadow strips and tree placement at hubs draw on the same classification ([[Planting Bed and Bioswale]], [[Wildflower Meadow Strip]]).
