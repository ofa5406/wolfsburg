# MiD 2017 Travel Survey

The national statistical bedrock under the project's demand model: **Mobilität in Deutschland 2017**, Germany's large-scale household travel survey (commissioned by the federal transport ministry) — hundreds of thousands of respondents recording who travels, how far, by what mode, at what times.

The project uses MiD as its trip-behaviour prior: per-person trip rates and modal shares, **adjusted for Wolfsburg's exceptional car dependency** to the working baseline of 62% car, 20% walking, 10% transit, 8% cycling ([[Modal Distribution Baseline]], [[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]). From these rates the 104,100 daily trips derive — which is how the demand model avoids the double-counting trap: person-groups carry survey trip rates rather than being summed as separate trip pools ([[Demand Basis]], [[Risk — Fleet Math Challenged]]).

Its limits are recorded with it: 2017 predates the Deutschlandticket, the e-bike boom and hybrid work, so the baseline is conservative about alternatives — the car share it reports is, if anything, the ceiling the project measures its shift against ([[Deutschlandticket Modal Shift]], [[Modal Shift]]).
