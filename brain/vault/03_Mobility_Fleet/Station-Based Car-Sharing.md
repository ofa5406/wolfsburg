# Station-Based Car-Sharing

Car-sharing's disciplined form: vehicles rented by the hour, picked up from and returned to a **fixed station** — in the Hub City, a hub. Station-based systems post the strongest evidence in the shared-mobility literature: the German Carsharing Association reports roughly **11 private cars replaced per shared car**, and peer-reviewed German studies find the effect robust for station-based sharing while remaining weak or insignificant for free-floating ([[Free-Floating Car-Sharing]]).

The mechanism is behavioural: a station-based car requires a small act of planning, so it substitutes for *ownership* rather than for walking or transit. Members treat it as the household's second (or only) car dissolved into the network — exactly the substitution the Hub City needs at scale.

The 175 [[Shared EV Car]] vehicles are station-based by design: they live at hubs, charge at hubs ([[EV Fleet Charging at Scale]]), and are booked through the [[Mobility as a Service]] layer. The choice is an evidence-led decision, not an aesthetic one — the project sites its car-sharing on the model with the proven replacement ratio, and the hub network supplies the stations for free.
