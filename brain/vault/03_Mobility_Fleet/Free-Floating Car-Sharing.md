# Free-Floating Car-Sharing

Car-sharing's spontaneous form: cars picked up and dropped anywhere inside a zone (ShareNow, Miles). It feels more convenient than [[Station-Based Car-Sharing]] and recruits more casual users — but its evidence is weaker where it counts. Claimed replacement ratios run from 8 up to 23 private cars per shared vehicle, yet peer-reviewed German research finds the ownership-reduction effect weak or statistically insignificant for free-floating fleets. Spontaneous access substitutes for *trips* (often transit and walking trips) more than for *ownership*.

The Hub City therefore declines the free-floating model for its car class: the 175 [[Shared EV Car]] fleet is hub-anchored. The decision costs a little convenience and buys three things — the proven substitution effect, orderly streets (no vehicles abandoned on pavements, the failure mode free-floating scooter fleets made famous), and a charging logistics problem that solves itself because every vehicle ends its booking at a hub with a charger ([[EV Fleet Charging at Scale]]).

Free-floating's real lesson survives in the [[Micro-Pod]]: spontaneity is delivered by autonomy instead — the vehicle comes to the rider, then returns itself to a dock.
