# Robotaxi Model

The autonomy pattern that actually works in 2026: **normal-speed cars on open roads, geographically gated, expanding city by city**. Waymo is the proof — roughly 450,000 paid rides per week across Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Austin, Atlanta and Miami with about 2,500 vehicles, now including freeway driving.

Two lessons travel from the robotaxi model to Wolfsburg. First, **speed matters**: the model succeeds at normal road speeds, unlike the 15 km/h campus shuttle that stalled ([[Low-Speed Shuttle Class]]). The Hub City's [[Micro-Pod]] and [[Shared EV Car]] classes assume road-speed autonomy for exactly this reason. Second, **one ride, one party is the wrong economics for a whole city**: Waymo's model adds vehicle-kilometres the way ride-hailing does. The Hub City takes robotaxi *technology* but pairs it with pooling ([[Ridepooling]]) and hub-anchored docking, so the automation dividend pays out in fewer vehicles, not more empty ones.

The counter-case is Cruise: shut down in December 2024 after a pedestrian incident and >$10B of losses — the standing argument for [[Phased Autonomy]].
