# Integrated Ticketing

The organisational keystone of [[Mobility as a Service]]: **one app, one payment, one journey** across every mode in the city. Helsinki's Whim proved it changes behaviour — over 2 million trips in its first year, ~6% of the population using it, 12% of users reporting they gave up a car because of it ([[Whim Helsinki]]).

Helsinki also teaches the harder lesson: integrated ticketing is **governance-hard, not tech-hard**. Whim worked because Finnish law forces transport operators to open their ticketing APIs; Whim's parent company nonetheless hit financial trouble. The single app exists only where regulation compels operators to plug into it.

The Hub City states this dependency out loud. Its fleet is operationally unified — one municipal-VW operator, so the multi-operator API problem largely dissolves — but regional rail, and any future private services, must be compelled into the ticket by policy. The brain records integrated ticketing as a **policy deliverable with a legislative precondition**, not a software feature: the [[Dispatch Optimiser]] can be bought, the open-API mandate must be won. [[Trip Chaining]] is what riders feel; integrated ticketing is what makes it feel like one system.
