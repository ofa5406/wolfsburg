# Commuter Corridor

The best possible geometry for pooled transport: many origins funnelling to **one destination at one time**. Pooling efficiency rises roughly logarithmically with demand density ([[Pooling Efficiency]]), so a corridor — thick, directional, predictable — pools far better than the same number of trips scattered across a city.

Wolfsburg's corridor is the movement between the residential districts and the [[VW Factory Gate]]: the [[VW Shift Wave]] concentrates ~10,000 workers into about an hour, in one direction, twice over as shifts swap. What looks like the fleet's stress test is actually its ideal operating case — vehicles are [[Pre-positioning|pre-positioned]] before the wave, run full in one direction, and carry the outgoing shift back, cancelling most [[Deadheading]].

The corridor argument reframes the scariest number in the project. Ten thousand workers in an hour are not served by ten thousand pods; they are served by high-capacity pooled modes — [[Autonomous Bus]] and [[Shuttle Pod]] — cycling a dense, reversible route ([[Shift Wave Capacity Check]]).
