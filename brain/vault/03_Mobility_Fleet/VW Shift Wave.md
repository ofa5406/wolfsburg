# VW Shift Wave

Wolfsburg's defining demand event: **~10,000 workers arriving at (or leaving) the Volkswagen plant within about one hour** at each shift change. It sounds like the fleet's nightmare and is argued as its strongest case, for three structural reasons: the wave is **predictable** (fixed shift times allow pre-positioning, eliminating dispatch latency), **concentrated** (one origin region, one destination — maximum pooling efficiency, which rises roughly logarithmically with demand density), and **directional-reversible** (the vehicles delivering shift A carry shift B home, minimising empty backhaul).

Most cities struggle with dispersed, unpredictable demand; Wolfsburg's factory clock synchronises demand into a corridor — the best possible ride-pooling geometry, already served commercially by VW's own MOIA in Hamburg. The wave is the proof framework's flagship simulation ([[VW Shift Wave Simulation]]) and the reason the project claims Wolfsburg as the ideal pilot city ([[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]).
