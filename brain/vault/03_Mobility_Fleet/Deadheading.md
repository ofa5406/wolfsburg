# Deadheading

Empty vehicle-kilometres: distance a shared vehicle travels with no passenger aboard, whether repositioning toward demand, returning from a drop-off, or heading to charge. Deadheading is shared mobility's tax — MOIA-type simulations put it at **14–28% of vehicle-kilometres** in directed-demand scenarios — and any honest fleet claim must carry it.

The project's demand geometry is its mitigation. Paired, reversible shift waves mean the vehicle that delivers workers collects the counter-shift rather than returning empty; pre-positioning during predictable troughs converts dead runs into scheduled logistics; and charging is folded into the naturally empty legs toward the L-hubs. Deadheading is also the argument *against* non-pooled robotaxi models — ride-hailing without pooling adds empty kilometres to the street, which is why the fleet contains no such class.
