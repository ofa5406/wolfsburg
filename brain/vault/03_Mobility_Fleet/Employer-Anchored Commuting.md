# Employer-Anchored Commuting

The proven pattern the Hub City generalises: large employers already run private mass-transit networks for their workers. Silicon Valley operates ~1,020 commuter buses for tech campuses; Apple's programme alone cost ~$35M for ~1,600 employees. Dedicated shift-worker shuttle services exist as an established market category. Where a big employer concentrates demand, shared commuting works — and the employer will pay for it, as a recruiting and retention tool.

Wolfsburg is the extreme case of the precondition: one employer, one gate, the densest predictable commuter wave in Germany ([[VW Shift Wave]]). What the Hub City adds is (a) **autonomy** — the shuttles run without drivers, fixing the economics that killed driver-based pooling ([[BerlKönig]]) — and (b) **civic generalisation**: the employer shuttle becomes the city's public fleet, serving residents and visitors between waves instead of parking at a depot.

The pattern also names the natural funding partner. [[Volkswagen]] already owns the operational know-how through [[MOIA]]; the project's pitch is that the company that filled the city with parked cars underwrites the system that replaces them.
