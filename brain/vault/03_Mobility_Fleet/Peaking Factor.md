# Peaking Factor

The share of a day's trips that lands in the busiest hour — the quiet number that decides whether a fleet plan is sane. The Hub City's is **9%**: 9,000 peak-hour trips against 100,000 daily ([[Peak Hour Demand]], [[Daily Trip Demand]]). Typical urban peak hours carry 8–12% of daily demand, so Wolfsburg's peak is normal, not pathological — despite containing a factory shift change.

The factor matters because fleets are sized to the peak, then spend the rest of the day underused. A city with a 20% peaking factor pays for capacity that idles 23 hours a day; at 9%, the [[Fleet]] earns its keep across the whole day, and the off-peak troughs become the charging windows the energy plan depends on ([[Charging Window Strategy]]).

At the vehicle level the peak works out to roughly 12 trips per vehicle in the peak hour fleet-wide — a trip every five minutes, easy for high-cycle micro-pods and e-bikes, while buses and shuttles carry the passenger volume through pooling ([[Trips per Vehicle per Day]]).
