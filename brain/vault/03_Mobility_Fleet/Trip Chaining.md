# Trip Chaining

How a journey actually crosses the Hub City: not one vehicle door to door, but a **chain of modes handed off at hubs**. A typical long trip reads: walk to the S-hub → [[Micro-Pod]] or [[E-Cargo Bike]] for the thin first mile → transfer at an M-hub to a [[Shuttle Pod]] or [[Autonomous Bus]] for the trunk → walk the final metres from the destination hub. Each link runs the mode that is cheapest and most comfortable at that distance; the [[Hub Tier System]] provides the physical splice points, and [[Intermodal Transfer]] design keeps the handoffs short and legible.

Chaining is what lets a 763-vehicle [[Fleet]] behave like a door-to-door system: small high-cycle vehicles solve the thin ends ([[Last Mile]]), big pooled vehicles carry the thick middle, and no mode is stretched into distances where it wastes capacity.

The experience must feel like *one* journey, not three bookings — which is why chaining depends on the [[Mobility as a Service]] layer: one app, one payment, one itinerary re-planned live if a link runs late.
