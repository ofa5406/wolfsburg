# Low-Speed Shuttle Class

The cautionary vehicle class: the 15–25 km/h autonomous "campus shuttle" (EasyMile EZ10, Navya Autonom) that dominated autonomy pilots through the late 2010s and then failed to scale. EasyMile logged 300+ sites in 30 countries — including driverless supervision at Paris 2024 Olympic venues — yet trial deployments largely did not convert into fleet orders; Navya sold ~200 vehicles and hit financial distress.

The class works where it was designed to work — fenced sites, campuses, controlled loops — and fails as city mobility, because 15 km/h cannot compete with a bicycle, let alone a car. The Hub City explicitly does **not** build its vision on this class: its pods and buses assume road-speed operation on the [[Robotaxi Model]] pattern, phased in under supervision ([[Phased Autonomy]]).

The note earns its place in the brain as an honesty marker. When a juror says "autonomous shuttles have been tried and they're toys," this is the class they mean — and the answer is that the project agrees, and bets instead on the production-grade, road-speed pooling vehicle that VW itself ships ([[MOIA]]).
