# Phased Autonomy

The project's honest position on self-driving: the Hub City assumes **supervised, geographically gated, gradually expanding autonomy** — never an overnight jump to Level 5. The evidence enforces humility in both directions. Waymo proves the [[Robotaxi Model]] works at scale (~450,000 paid rides a week by late 2025), but took a decade and billions to get there. Cruise proves the downside: one safety failure and a regulatory crisis ended a multi-billion-dollar programme in months. And the slow campus shuttle class largely failed to convert trials into fleets ([[Low-Speed Shuttle Class]]).

The Hub City therefore phases its fleet: early years run with safety drivers and remote supervision on fixed corridors — exactly how [[MOIA]] introduces the ID. Buzz AD in Hamburg — before expanding the driverless envelope zone by zone. The [[Hub Tier System]] supports this: fixed hub-to-hub links are the easiest possible operational design domain, far simpler than free-range robotaxi service.

Autonomy levels themselves are catalogued in [[Vehicle Automation Levels]]; the argument for why phased autonomy still transforms the city is the fleet economics — removing the driver is what makes dense pooling financially viable ([[BerlKönig]]).
