# Defaults Drive Subscription

The strongest nudge in the transport-behaviour toolkit, measured: in a transit-subscription experiment, **61.9% of participants subscribed when the pass was the pre-selected default**, versus far fewer under opt-in. People overwhelmingly keep whatever option arrives pre-chosen — in mobility as everywhere else.

The complementary evidence sharpens the lesson: social-identity labelling ("you're a sustainable traveler") measurably increases transit use, while pure subsidies fail to stick — a randomised trial found free transit raised ridership only while the subsidy lasted, with behaviour reverting afterwards ([[Free Transit Reverts]]).

The Hub City's application is concrete. Every VW onboarding, every new rental contract, every residential move inside the zones arrives with **hub membership and the mobility subscription pre-loaded** — opting out is possible, opting in is never required ([[Behaviour Change]]). Combined with the restructuring's mass disruption of routine ([[Moments of Change Evidence]]), the default does silently what persuasion campaigns fail to do loudly ([[Mobility as a Service]]).
