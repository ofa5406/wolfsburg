# MaaS Is Governance-Hard

The lesson under the Whim story: Mobility as a Service is **governance-hard, not tech-hard**. Helsinki's integrated app existed only because Finnish transport law *forces* operators to open their ticketing APIs; without that mandate, every operator hoards its own ticket and the "one app for everything" promise dies in contract negotiations. Even with the law, Whim's parent MaaS Global ran into financial trouble — the software was never the bottleneck; the institutional plumbing was ([[Whim Year One Numbers]]).

The Hub City therefore books its MaaS layer as a **policy dependency, not a feature**. The project's position is unusually favourable — one municipal-VW fleet operator dissolves most of the multi-operator problem internally ([[Integrated Ticketing]]) — but regional rail and any future private services still require an open-ticketing mandate to join the single journey.

Recording this dependency explicitly is part of the proof discipline: the jury sees the system's operating conditions named, priced and assigned, not assumed away ([[Proof Framework]]).
