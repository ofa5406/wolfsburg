# German Households Spend 233 Euro Monthly on Cars

The household ledger, from the federal statistics office: German households spend on average **€233 a month on their cars against €33 on public transport** — a seven-to-one ratio, before counting the societal costs the driver never sees ([[Societal Cost per Car 5000 Euro]]).

The number anchors the personal economic case for the Hub City. A household that trades ownership for access keeps most of that €233: no depreciation, insurance, maintenance, fuel or parking, in exchange for per-trip or subscription pricing through the [[Mobility as a Service]] layer. Bremen's data shows car-sharing households drive less than half the city average — the spending falls with the ownership, not just the badge ([[Bremen 16-to-1 Substitution]]).

In a company town facing pay cuts and job insecurity ([[VW Cuts 35000 Jobs]]), "keep €150+ a month in your pocket" is an argument that lands where liveability rhetoric does not ([[Access over Ownership]]).
