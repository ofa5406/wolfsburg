# Millennials Still Want Cars

The counter-evidence the brain keeps on purpose: a German millennial survey found a **strong stated desire to own a car — often a combustion one**. The attachment has not vanished; 66% of owners plan to keep their cars longer for financial reasons, and aspiration lags the behavioural decline documented in the licensing and usage data ([[Young Germans Drive Less]]).

Two readings coexist. The pessimistic one: culture is stickier than infrastructure, and stated preference will reassert itself whenever incomes allow ([[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]]). The optimistic one: stated preference is answered in the world as it is — where owning a car is the price of full participation, of course people want one. Berlin's 45% car-free households state a different preference because they live in different conditions ([[Berlin 45 Percent Car-Free Households]]).

The project plans for both readings: it never requires Wolfsburgers to stop *wanting* cars — only to find, trip by trip, that the hub system is easier ([[Behaviour Change]]). Desire can lag adoption indefinitely; the system works on behaviour, and lets identity follow at its own pace.
