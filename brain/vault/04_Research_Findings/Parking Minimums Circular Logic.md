# Parking Minimums Circular Logic

[[Donald Shoup]]'s diagnosis of how parking oversupply reproduces itself: cities mandate minimum parking per building; free, abundant parking makes driving the easy default; surveys then observe high parking demand; the observed demand justifies raising the minimums. **The demand the mandates claim to serve is manufactured by the mandates themselves.**

The loop has teeth: parking requirements can act as an impact fee more than **ten times** all other development fees combined, raising housing costs and pushing buildings apart until walking becomes impractical — sprawl as a by-product of storage rules ([[Parking Minimums]]).

The finding matters strategically because it locates the intervention point. The Hub City does not fight traffic at the street; it breaks the loop at its source, replacing the mandated-storage organising element with the shared-mobility hub ([[From Parking City to Hub City]]). When parking stops being free and mandatory, the "demand" that justified it deflates — the same mechanism as [[Induced Demand]], run in reverse.
