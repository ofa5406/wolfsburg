# Cars Parked 95 Percent of the Time

The private car's guilty secret: it sits parked for roughly **95% of its life**. An hour of driving a day is an optimistic average; the other twenty-three hours the machine occupies dwelling-sized land at both ends of its route and depreciates in place.

The figure powers two of the project's core arguments. Spatially, it means the parking city stores idleness: nearly all car-dedicated land serves vehicles that are doing nothing ([[Car-Dedicated Land]]). Operationally, it defines the efficiency headroom that shared fleets harvest — a [[Fleet]] vehicle in motion most of the day does the work of many parked private cars, which is why the ITF's Lisbon simulation could serve a whole city's trips with a few percent of its fleet ([[ITF Lisbon Study]], [[Vehicle Utilisation]]).

One number, both halves of the thesis: the waste of the old system and the mechanism of the new one.
