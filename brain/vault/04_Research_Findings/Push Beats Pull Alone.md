# Push Beats Pull Alone

The convergent finding of decades of modal-shift research: **pull measures alone rarely move people out of cars.** Better buses, new bike lanes and shiny hubs, offered while driving stays cheap and easy, mostly recruit people who were already close to switching. The heavy lifting is done by push measures — parking supply cuts, access restrictions, pricing — but push only survives politically when a credible pull alternative already exists.

The evidence is consistent across instruments: Nottingham's workplace parking levy cut car commuting 6.6% *because* its revenue visibly built the tram; Ghent's circulation plan collapsed car share from 55% to 27% *because* cycling infrastructure absorbed the shift ([[Ghent Circulation Plan]]); parking pricing without supply reduction merely relocates parking ([[Parking Relocation Effect]]).

The Hub City is architecture-as-sequencing: every hub opens (pull) before the parking it replaces closes (push), so the alternative is never a promise — it is standing at the kerb ([[Push and Pull Measures]], [[Implementation Timeline]]).
