# Moments of Change Evidence

Most travel is not chosen — it is habit, executed automatically within a stable context. The research finding that unlocks change: habits become rewritable when their **context is disrupted**. Moving home, changing jobs, having a child, retiring — these "windows of opportunity" are when interventions toward sustainable travel actually stick; persuasion at any other time mostly bounces off the routine.

People relocating from suburban to urban contexts measurably reduce car use and increase walking, cycling and transit — the *moment* does the work the message cannot. Even forced disruptions (roadworks, closures, plant reorganisations) convert into behaviour change when an attractive alternative is present *during* the disruption ([[Forced Disruption as Opportunity]]).

Wolfsburg is about to experience a mass moment of change: VW's restructuring re-times shifts, redeploys workers and rewrites thousands of daily routines at once ([[VW Cuts 35000 Jobs]], [[Golf Moves to Puebla]]). The [[Moments of Change]] concept turns this finding into the project's timing strategy — launch the hub offer into the disruption, not after it.
