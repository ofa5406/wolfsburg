# Fleet Energy in Tens of MWh

The order-of-magnitude answer to "can the grid feed the fleet": a study of 15,000 EVs serving Austin's metro passenger demand estimated **~806 MWh/day** of charging energy — the daily electricity of ~27,400 US homes. Wolfsburg's fleet is 763 vehicles, a twentieth of that count, and skews small — e-bikes and micro-pods sip a fraction of a car's energy. A rough scale-down lands the Hub City fleet in the **tens of MWh per day**: real, budgetable, and modest at city scale.

The number's job is deflationary. Fleet electrification sounds like a grid crisis until it is quantified; tens of MWh against a city hosting Germany's largest factory is an engineering task, not a showstopper ([[EV Fleet Charging at Scale]]).

The favourable structure matters as much as the size: charging concentrates at six L-hub depots on a schedule the operator controls ([[Charging Window Strategy]], [[Plan in MWh Not MW]]) — the gentlest possible load shape, far kinder than the same trips charged at thousands of private home sockets.
