# Transit-Anchored Hubs Perform Better

The siting evidence from Minneapolis's 2019 pilot — twelve hubs, three months, each combining a bus stop, bench, bike/scooter parking and wayfinding with travel times: **64% of users said the improvements made them more likely to use the hub's transport options**, and hubs anchored on transit stops clearly out-performed those placed only on bike infrastructure. Proximity and a *visual* link to the transit stop were decisive.

Two Wolfsburg decisions inherit this evidence directly. Hub siting: every L- and M-hub sits on the autonomous-bus or shuttle trunk network, never on micromobility alone ([[Hub Tier System]]). And information design: wayfinding that shows *travel times to real destinations* — not just a map — is what converted intention into use, which is the brief for the Zone 2 real-time terminal ([[Real-time Info Terminal]], [[Zone 2 — Threshold]]).

The 64% figure is stated-preference from a short pilot; the brain quotes it as directional evidence for siting logic, corroborated by every precedent network's transit-adjacent placement ([[Bremen mobil.punkt]]).
