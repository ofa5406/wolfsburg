# Young Germans Drive Less

The demographic tailwind: **18-to-29-year-old Germans increasingly forgo car ownership**, especially in cities; fewer young adults obtain licences at all, car use among young adults has fallen since ~2000 with rising multimodality, and the historic gender gap has closed from above — young men reduced car travel toward young women's levels. Car-free households are far more common in cities over 100,000 people, and the decisive determinants are **quality transit and cycling infrastructure** — where the alternative is good, going car-free follows.

Berlin is the destination state: roughly **45% of households own no car** ([[Berlin 45 Percent Car-Free Households]]).

The honest counterweight travels attached: surveys still find many German millennials *want* to own a car, often with a combustion engine ([[Millennials Still Want Cars]]). Attitude lags behaviour — which is exactly what the infrastructure determinant predicts: preference follows provision. For Wolfsburg the finding sets the design bar. The counter-trend is real but conditional; the Hub City must supply the infrastructure quality that makes car-free the path of least resistance ([[Modal Shift]], [[Behaviour Change]]).
