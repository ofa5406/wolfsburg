# Free-Floating Substitution Evidence Is Weak

The honesty note inside the substitution story: peer-reviewed German research finds car-sharing's ownership-reduction effect **robust for station-based systems but weak or statistically insignificant for free-floating ones**, despite marketing claims of up to 23 cars replaced per vehicle. Spontaneous, drop-anywhere access appears to substitute for individual *trips* — often transit and walking trips — more than for car *ownership*, which is the variable that actually releases parking land.

The finding is one of the quiet design decisions of the Hub City made visible: its 175 shared cars are hub-anchored precisely because that is the variant with defensible evidence ([[Station-Based Car-Sharing]], [[Free-Floating Car-Sharing]]). A jury that probes the substitution ratios should find the project already standing on the strong side of the literature.

It also models the brain's epistemic standard: every hero number carries its caveat, and contested evidence is recorded as contested rather than averaged away ([[One Shared Car Replaces 11 Private Cars]]).
