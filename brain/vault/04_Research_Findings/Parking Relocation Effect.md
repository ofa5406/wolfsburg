# Parking Relocation Effect

The caveat that shapes the whole parking strategy: raising the *price* of parking without reducing its *supply* often just makes drivers hunt for cheaper spots. Trips relocate rather than switch mode, which makes price measures look deceptively weak in evaluations.

Real modal shift requires shrinking the **amount and convenience** of parking, not only its cost. Daily parking charges of €1–3 cut car-kilometres by only ~0.3–3% at city scale — and the effect strengthens markedly when capacity reduction accompanies the price.

This is the strongest single argument for the project's physical thesis. A pricing scheme leaves the parking city intact and waiting to refill; **replacing the spaces with hubs removes the option to relocate into**. The Hub City is, in behavioural terms, a supply-side parking policy with the substitute bundled in at the same kerb ([[Push and Pull Measures]]). Price becomes the fine-tuning instrument, not the transformation.
