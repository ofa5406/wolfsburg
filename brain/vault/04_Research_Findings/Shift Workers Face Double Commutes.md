# Shift Workers Face Double Commutes

The equity finding that writes the project's hardest design brief: transit thins out at night, and **late-shift transit commutes can take about twice as long as driving** — for exactly the workers with the least schedule flexibility. Applied to Wolfsburg, where ~10,000 VW workers move per shift within an hour, a hub system that only works at 8 a.m. would abandon its core constituency at 10 p.m. and hand the backlash its story: the war on the worker.

The finding generalises into the equity table the personas are built from: carers chaining childcare–school–work trips, disabled and older residents who cannot cycle, rural in-commuters with no alternative at the origin ([[Equity of the Post-Car Transition]], [[Anna]]). The principle: target restrictions where alternatives are strong; offer generous alternatives — night service timed to shift changes, accessible on-demand vehicles, edge park-and-ride — where car dependence is structural ([[Demand-Responsive Transit]]).

Equity here is not virtue signalling; it is the political shield. The system must demonstrably serve the night-shift worker *better* than the car did, or it will not survive contact with the town ([[VW Shift Wave]]).
