# ITF Lisbon Study

The most authoritative simulation of exactly what the Hub City proposes: the OECD/International Transport Forum modelled Lisbon with its entire car and bus system replaced by shared, self-driving fleets. The results define the theoretical envelope — **the same trips delivered with as little as 3% of the existing car fleet**, essentially all on-street parking freed, and each shared vehicle doing the work of about ten private cars ([[One-for-Ten Substitution]]). A follow-up study of Helsinki confirmed the Lisbon results in a second city.

The study's power for the project is as a **ceiling, not a claim**. Wolfsburg's 763 vehicles for ~100,000 daily trips sits far inside the Lisbon envelope — conservative by an order of magnitude — which converts the scariest question ("can so few vehicles serve a city?") into arithmetic the jury can check ([[Fleet Sizing]], [[Trips per Vehicle per Day]]).

Its mechanism is [[Vehicle Utilisation]]: vehicles in motion most of the day instead of parked 95% of it ([[Cars Parked 95 Percent of the Time]]).
