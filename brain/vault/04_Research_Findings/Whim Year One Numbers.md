# Whim Year One Numbers

The adoption evidence for Mobility as a Service, from its flagship deployment: Helsinki's Whim app logged **over 2 million trips in its first year** (launched November 2017), reached **~6% of the city's population** — concentrated among 18-to-40-year-olds — and **12% of users reported the app prompted them to give up a car**, with a similar share planning to.

Twelve percent is the number to hold onto: a phone app, with no street rebuilt, dissolved car ownership for one user in eight. It demonstrates that the *interface* layer of mobility — one app, one payment, one itinerary — has behavioural force of its own, independent of infrastructure ([[Mobility as a Service]], [[Integrated Ticketing]]).

The caveat travels with the number: Whim's parent company later hit financial trouble, and the Finnish open-API law was the precondition for the app existing at all ([[MaaS Is Governance-Hard]]). The Hub City quotes Helsinki as proof of behavioural effect, not of business model ([[Whim Helsinki]]).
