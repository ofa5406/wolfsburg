# Barcelona Health Dividend

The public-health case for taking space from cars, modelled by ISGlobal for Barcelona's full superblock programme: **~667 premature deaths prevented every year** — 291 from cleaner air (NO₂), 163 from reduced noise, 117 from a cooler heat island — plus roughly **200 extra days of life expectancy per resident** and **€1.7 billion a year** in economic savings, mostly health costs avoided ([[Barcelona Superblocks]]).

The framing power of the finding is that it recasts street design as medicine. Removing car space is not an aesthetic preference or a culture-war position; it is a quantified health intervention with a mortality line, comparable to vaccination programmes in its arithmetic.

For a jury, the companion figure may matter more: the dividend does not require going car-free ([[25 Percent Traffic Cut Saves 200 Lives]]). The Hub City's claim is proportionate — a credible, phased reduction in traffic buys measurable years of life across the whole population ([[Post-Car City]]).
