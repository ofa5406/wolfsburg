# Forced Disruption as Opportunity

The uncomfortable cousin of voluntary life-event change: **involuntary disruptions — roadworks, bridge closures, strikes, factory reorganisations — also break travel habits open**, and research shows they convert into lasting behaviour change when an attractive alternative is offered *while the disruption is live*. Commuters forced to try the alternative discover it is workable; a meaningful share never switch back.

The condition is simultaneity. An alternative launched after the disruption ends meets re-settled habits; one available during the window inherits the traffic. This sharpens the project's programming: hub services, shift shuttles and cycle routes must be operational *when* Wolfsburg's plant reorganisation scrambles commute patterns, not promised for afterwards ([[Moments of Change Evidence]]).

It also reframes construction itself. Building the hub network will disrupt streets — and each closure is a live experiment in car-free routing, exactly the tactical-trial logic the consent strategy runs on ([[Tactical Urbanism]]). The disruption budget and the behaviour-change budget are the same line item, spent twice.
