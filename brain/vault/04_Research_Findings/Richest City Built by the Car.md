# Richest City Built by the Car

The symbolic charge the project runs on: Wolfsburg is **Germany's richest city by GDP per capita (~€120,000)** — wealth built entirely by and for the automobile, in a city founded in 1938 for the sole purpose of building cars ([[Wolfsburg]], [[Volkswagen]]).

The statistic completes the project's rhetorical triad alongside the record car density ([[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]) and the world's largest car factory ([[Worlds Largest Car Factory]]): nowhere on Earth is the twentieth-century bargain — prosperity in exchange for a car-shaped city — more purely embodied. Which is exactly what makes the site irresistible: **there is no more symbolic place to design a post-private-car city than the hometown of Volkswagen.** If the car's birthplace can reorganise itself around shared mobility, the argument is settled everywhere milder ([[Post-Car City in VW's Hometown]]).

The wealth is also practical: a city with this fiscal base, and a company betting its future on mobility services ([[NEW AUTO Strategy]]), can actually afford the transformation it symbolises ([[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]).
