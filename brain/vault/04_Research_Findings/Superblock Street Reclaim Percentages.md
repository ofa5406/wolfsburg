# Superblock Street Reclaim Percentages

Barcelona's measured answer to "how much street can people get back": superblocks reclaim **up to 60–70% of street space** for pedestrians and public life while removing only about a quarter of through-traffic. The Eixample rollout plan converts **one street in three** into green space, adds 21 new plazas, and targets green space within **200 metres of every home**; early superblocks recorded NO₂ drops around 33% ([[Barcelona Superblocks]]).

The asymmetry is the finding: a modest reduction in vehicle movement buys a majority share of the surface, because so much street area serves parking, over-wide lanes and turning geometry rather than actual flow. Wolfsburg's zone structure applies the same logic at city-sector scale — filter the through-traffic ([[Filtered Permeability]]), and most of the carriageway becomes negotiable ([[Street-Space Reallocation]]).

The 200-metre green-access target also quietly endorses the project's S-hub grain: a 150–250 m service radius is the same "everything within a short walk" discipline, applied to mobility ([[S-Hub]]).
