# Paris 13 Percent Trips 50 Percent Space

The spatial-justice equation that justified Paris's transformation: cars carry about **13% of trips but occupy roughly 50% of street space**. Half the public realm serves an eighth of the movement — the rest of the city's journeys, on foot, bike and transit, compress into what remains.

The number reframes street-space reallocation from a taking to a **rebalancing**: matching the division of public land to how people actually move is not anti-car policy, it is arithmetic. On this basis Paris committed to removing ~60,000 on-street spaces (72% of its stock) and freeing 500 streets ([[Paris 15-Minute City]]).

Wolfsburg's version of the equation is starker — the highest car density in Germany means the space claim is even more lopsided ([[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]). The [[Car-Land Composite Map]] computes the local ratio; the [[Street Transformation Sections]] show what the rebalanced street looks like, metre by metre ([[Street-Space Reallocation]]).
