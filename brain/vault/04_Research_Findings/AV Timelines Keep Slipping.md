# AV Timelines Keep Slipping

The pattern in the most authoritative industry forecast: McKinsey's biannual autonomous-vehicle survey expects large-scale global Level-4 robotaxi rollout around **2030**, ~37% of new cars with advanced autonomy by **2035**, and Level-3+ in up to 80% of European new-car sales by **2040** — but each edition's timelines have **slipped one to two years** against the previous survey, with private urban AV pilots pushed toward ~2032.

The finding is not that autonomy fails; it is that autonomy is systematically **later than predicted**, cycle after cycle. Any plan whose viability requires an AV date to hold is structurally fragile.

The Hub City is therefore engineered to be slippage-proof: Phase 1 runs on supervised vehicles and safety drivers using today's technology; the hub network, cycling infrastructure, and land reclamation deliver value with zero autonomy ([[25 Percent Traffic Cut Saves 200 Lives]]); autonomy upgrades the economics when it arrives ([[Phased Autonomy]], [[Implementation Timeline]]). The design treats Level 5 as a bonus, never a dependency.
