# Parking Free for 99 Percent of Trips

The core statistic of [[Donald Shoup]]'s *The High Cost of Free Parking* (2005): in the United States, parking is free to the driver for **about 99% of car trips**. The cost does not vanish — it is folded into rents, groceries, wages and taxes, paid by everyone including people who never drive.

"Free" parking is therefore a massive hidden subsidy to driving, and the mechanism that makes [[Car Dependency]] feel natural: if storing the car costs nothing at either end of the trip, the car wins every comparison. Shoup's companion estimate makes the scale vivid — the land and construction value of all US parking exceeds the value of all the cars, possibly all the roads ([[61 Square Metres per Parking Space]]).

For the Hub City, the finding justifies the pricing half of the transformation: hub-based mobility is honestly priced per use, while the invisible parking subsidy is withdrawn along with the spaces themselves ([[Parking Reform]]).
