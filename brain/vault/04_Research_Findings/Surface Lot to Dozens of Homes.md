# Surface Lot to Dozens of Homes

What reclaimed parking actually becomes, quantified from built projects: a single surface parking lot can hold **dozens of homes**; Houston's courtyard-infill pattern puts 3–8 houses on each former lot; London's parking reform cut residential provision by ~49%, redirecting the land and construction cost into housing itself. Study after study finds that removing parking minimums measurably increases affordable-housing supply ([[Parking Over-Provision by Mandate]]).

The finding turns the project's land dividend from rhetoric into programme. Wolfsburg's freed lots are not destined to be vague "open space" — they are building plots, already publicly located, already served by streets and pipes, in a city that needs homes near the plant more than it needs car storage ([[Reclaimed Land]]).

The Hub City's version is layered: L-hubs carry housing above their mobility floors ([[L-Hub]]), while dissolved surface lots between hubs become the fine grain — courtyard housing, gardens, and the plazas of the [[Public-Space Dividend]].
