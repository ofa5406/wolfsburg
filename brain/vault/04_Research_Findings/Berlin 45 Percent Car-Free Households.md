# Berlin 45 Percent Car-Free Households

The German proof that car-free is already normal: in Berlin, roughly **45% of households own no car**. Not activists, not an experiment — nearly half the capital's households, living ordinary lives on transit, bikes, feet and occasional shared cars.

The number's rhetorical work is normalisation. Wolfsburg sits at the opposite pole — the highest car density in Germany ([[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]) — and the gap between the two cities is not geography or wealth but **infrastructure and form**: Berlin's density, transit and cycling networks make the car optional; Wolfsburg's dispersed, parking-saturated fabric makes it compulsory ([[Car Dependency]]).

The Hub City's pitch borrows the benchmark: car-light living is not a sacrifice being invented for Wolfsburg; it is the established norm in German cities that built the alternative. The project's job is to compress into a decade the infrastructure advantage Berlin accumulated over a century — with the hub network as the accelerant ([[Post-Car City]], [[Young Germans Drive Less]]).
