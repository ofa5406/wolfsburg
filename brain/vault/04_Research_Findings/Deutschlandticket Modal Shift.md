# Deutschlandticket Modal Shift

The national-scale rebuttal to "Germans will never switch": the €49 **Deutschlandticket** produced, in its first year, **+16% local public-transport ridership, +30% rail journeys, and a 4.7% drop in total transport CO₂ (~6.7 million tonnes)** — with about 8% of users entirely new to public transport (MCC Berlin / PIK evaluation).

One affordable, radically *simple* ticket — no tariff zones, no route planning anxiety — moved measurable percentages of the most car-attached population in Europe within twelve months. The mechanism echoes the MaaS evidence: reducing the cognitive cost of the alternative shifts behaviour almost as powerfully as improving the physical one ([[Whim Year One Numbers]], [[Integrated Ticketing]]).

Paired with the Frankfurt acceptability research — support for car-reduction measures is "surprisingly high" when local benefits are tangible, and push measures are the most effective lever — it arms the project's answer to the culture objection: German behaviour is already moving; the offer just has to be good, simple and visible ([[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]], [[Push Beats Pull Alone]]).
