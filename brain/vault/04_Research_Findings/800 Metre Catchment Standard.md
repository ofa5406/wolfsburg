# 800 Metre Catchment Standard

The international reference number for hub spacing: CoMoUK — the UK's national shared-transport body and author of the only hub accreditation scheme — evaluates candidate sites by population within **800 metres, about a 10-minute walk**, and the UK government's placemaking guidance repeats the same standard for masterplanning. Beyond that distance, the first leg stops being walked and the interchange loses its catchment.

The Hub City treats 800 m as a **ceiling and over-delivers beneath it**: L-hubs anchor the full 700–1000 m interchange catchment; M-hubs tighten to 300–500 m; S-hubs to 150–250 m, a two-to-three-minute walk ([[Hub Tier System]], [[Service Radius]]). The radii are nested, so missing your nearest hub means the next is one block on — the network degrades gracefully.

The defensible claim this finding underwrites: the project's spacing is not invented; it is the published standard, applied and then deliberately exceeded because a *post-car* district makes walking the default first leg ([[Walking Catchment]], [[Hub Coverage]]).
