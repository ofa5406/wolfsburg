# Hub Component Ladder

The published typology that the project's three tiers map onto: UK government / CoMoUK guidance sorts mobility hubs into four ascending types — **1–2 modes with core components** (low-density residential), **1–2 modes with extra components** (high streets: +WiFi, water, cycle repair), **3+ modes with more components** (suburban centres, rail stations: +toilets, baby-change), and **3+ modes with commercial uses** (town centres, universities: retail and community space).

The correspondence to the Hub City is nearly one-to-one: S-hubs are the "core" type, M-hubs the "more components" type, L-hubs the "commercial uses" type with housing added above ([[Hub Tier System]], [[S-Hub]], [[M-Hub]], [[L-Hub]]). Vienna's WienMobil network demonstrates the same ladder in operation — bike-share held constant as the baseline element, capability layered upward by site ([[Vienna WienMobil]]).

The finding's rhetorical job: when the jury asks *why these three tiers with these elements*, the answer is national guidance and a running network, not taste ([[Hub Toolpalette]]).
