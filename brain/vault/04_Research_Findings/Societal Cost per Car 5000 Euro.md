# Societal Cost per Car 5000 Euro

The peer-reviewed German number for what a private car costs everyone else: Gössling et al. (2022) calculated the lifetime cost of driving and found **society pays €4,674 per year for a small car (Opel Corsa, 41% of its true cost) and €5,273 for a large one (Mercedes GLC, 29%)** — pollution, noise, crashes, land and climate costs the driver never sees on an invoice.

Older EU-wide work points the same direction: external costs of ~€2,400–4,000 per car per year, summing to roughly £303 billion annually across the EU (TU Dresden, 2012 — date it when quoting).

Multiplied across Wolfsburg's record car stock ([[Wolfsburg Highest Car Density]]), the invisible subsidy runs to hundreds of millions of euros a year. The finding reverses the affordability framing: the shared system is not the expensive alternative — the private system is, with its costs laundered through health budgets, insurance pools and public land ([[German Households Spend 233 Euro Monthly on Cars]]).
