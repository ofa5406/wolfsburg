# MOIA Hannover 60 Percent Pooled

The field number for pooling's real-world ceiling: in Hannover, **around 60% of MOIA trips at peak times are pooled** — strangers sharing a vehicle heading the same way — across a 204 km² service area including outer districts. Hamburg's larger operation (~450 electric six-seaters, Europe's biggest ride-pooling service) demonstrates the same model at scale ([[MOIA]]).

Sixty percent matters because pooling is where shared mobility's efficiency actually lives: a pooled trip removes a vehicle-movement, not just a parked car. MOIA's own simulation research established that pooling efficiency rises roughly logarithmically with demand density ([[Pooling Efficiency]]) — and Hannover's peak is exactly the dense, directional condition under which the [[VW Shift Wave]] operates, at several times the intensity.

For the project's credibility the provenance is the argument: these numbers come from **Volkswagen's own mobility subsidiary**, operating profitably-adjacent ride-pooling in two German cities. The Hub City asks Wolfsburg to adopt an operation its hometown company already runs ([[Shuttle Pod]]).
