# Free Transit Reverts

The cautionary randomised-controlled trial: in King County, means-tested **free transit raised ridership only while the subsidy ran — once it ended, behaviour reverted** to baseline. The habit had rented the new mode, not bought it.

The mechanism is the same one that makes habits hard to break in the first place: a price incentive changes the calculation without changing the *context*; remove the incentive and the unchanged context reasserts the old routine. Lasting shift requires permanent alteration of the choice environment — less parking, better service, closer destinations — with incentives as accelerant, never as foundation ([[Behaviour Change]], [[Push and Pull Measures]]).

For the Hub City this finding ranks the investments: the free introductory offers and trial passes that launch each phase are marketing, useful and forgettable; the load-bearing changes are physical — parking dissolved into hubs, the alternative standing at the kerb permanently ([[Parking Relocation Effect]]). Money buys trial; only geometry buys habit.
