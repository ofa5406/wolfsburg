# LTN 46 Percent Internal Traffic Drop

The hardest number in the filtered-permeability evidence: a study of **46 Low-Traffic Neighbourhoods across 11 London boroughs found internal traffic fell ~46.9% on average — with no corresponding rise on boundary roads.** The traffic did not relocate; most of it evaporated ([[Disappearing Traffic Study]], [[Traffic Evaporation]]).

The same evidence base answers the emergency-services objection: services are consulted before implementation and typically report **no change in response times** — residents, deliveries, refuse and ambulances retain full access; only *through*-traffic is filtered, increasingly by ANPR camera rather than physical barrier ([[Freight and Emergency Access]], [[Modal Filter]]).

This is the project's prepared one-slide rebuttal for the most reliable attack on the zone structure: *"46% internal reduction, no boundary increase, no emergency delay"* — three claims, one citation, against an objection that otherwise runs on anecdote ([[Five-Zone Model]], [[LTN Active Travel Evidence]]). The perception problem remains real and is handled as politics, not data ([[Imposition Without Consent]]).
