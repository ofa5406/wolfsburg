# Wolfsburg Privileged Identity Study

Academic evidence about Wolfsburg specifically: a study of company towns and the "governmentality of desired identities" found VW employees were **constituted as a privileged, "successful and happy" elite, with belonging policed against outsiders**. The car is not merely the town's product; it is the badge of membership in that in-group — which is why proposing to design the private car *away* can register as an attack on identity, not a mobility upgrade ([[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]]).

The town's textbook company-town structure amplifies the effect: one employer shaping housing, recreation and identity, "reaching beyond the workplace into workers' private and social lives" — roughly the entire working population tied to VW or its partners ([[Volkswagen]], [[Worlds Largest Car Factory]]).

The project's response is architectural to its narrative: the post-car city must be framed as the *next chapter of the same privileged identity* — Wolfsburg as the world's reference project for VW's mobility future ([[NEW AUTO Strategy]], [[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]) — so that pride in the town transfers to the transformation instead of mobilising against it.
