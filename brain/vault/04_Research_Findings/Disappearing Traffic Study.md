# Disappearing Traffic Study

The empirical backbone of [[Traffic Evaporation]]: Cairns, Atkins and Goodwin's landmark synthesis *Disappearing Traffic? The Story So Far* (2002) reviewed ~70 cases of road-space reallocation across Europe and found that, on average, **a significant share of the displaced traffic could not be found on surrounding streets — it had evaporated**. Trips consolidated, re-timed, switched mode or simply stopped being made; the predicted gridlock on parallel routes systematically failed to materialise.

The study is the direct counter to the most reliable objection every car-space scheme faces: "the traffic will just clog the next street over." The evidence says networks adapt — driver behaviour is elastic in both directions, which is also why new road capacity fills up ([[Induced Demand]]; the same elasticity, run forward).

For Wolfsburg's zone entrances and filtered streets, the study licenses confidence with honesty: some displacement occurs and boundary streets must be designed for it, but the wholesale relocation of traffic that critics predict is not what seventy cases show ([[Filtered Permeability]]).
