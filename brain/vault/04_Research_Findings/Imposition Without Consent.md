# Imposition Without Consent

Why car-reduction schemes blow up, distilled from the UK's LTN wave: many schemes were imposed rapidly under COVID emergency powers, bypassing consultation. A national survey found **45% support versus 21% opposition — but 58% of residents did not know they lived in an LTN at all**. Low awareness plus perceived imposition proved politically fatal even where the policy itself polled positively: Ealing scrapped seven LTNs after boundary-road opposition hit 82–92%, driven by the *perception* of displaced traffic regardless of measurements ([[LTN Active Travel Evidence]]).

Berlin adds the referendum lesson: the 2023 climate-neutrality vote won 50.9% yes but failed its turnout quorum — a vocal minority is not a durable majority ([[Car Identity Predicts Resistance]]).

The successful cities inverted the pattern: Stockholm trialled its congestion charge first and watched acceptance *rise* with experienced benefits; Ghent delivered in one weekend and the responsible alderman was re-elected ([[Ghent Circulation Plan]]). The consent recipe the project adopts: reversible trials, fast visible benefits, revenue reinvested locally, co-design with VW and the Works Council — never letting imposition become the story ([[Tactical Urbanism]]).
