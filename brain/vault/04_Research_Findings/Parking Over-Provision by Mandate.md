# Parking Over-Provision by Mandate

German, Dutch and Swiss minimum-parking rules have been found to produce **50–100% more parking than is actually needed** — the reclaimable land is *surplus by design*, not scraped from genuine demand. Removing minimums measurably increases affordable-housing supply; London's reform cut residential parking provision by ~49% (0.76 fewer spaces per unit), releasing land and cost directly into housing ([[Surface Lot to Dozens of Homes]]).

The finding blunts the sharpest objection to the project — "but people need that parking." A large fraction of the stock exists because a formula demanded it, and the German legal instrument (the Land-level *Stellplatzverordnung*) is already being relaxed in several cities ([[Parking Reform]]).

For the Hub City this defines the easy first phase: the surplus half of the parking stock can convert with no mobility substitute at all, while the genuinely used remainder is what the hub network must absorb ([[Implementation Timeline]]).
