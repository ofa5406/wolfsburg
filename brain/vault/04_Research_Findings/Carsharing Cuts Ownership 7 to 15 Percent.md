# Carsharing Cuts Ownership 7 to 15 Percent

The city-scale version of the substitution evidence: studies of German cities find car-sharing reduces private-car ownership by roughly **7–15% per provider** in served areas. Where the per-vehicle ratios ([[One Shared Car Replaces 11 Private Cars]], [[Bremen 16-to-1 Substitution]]) describe what each shared car achieves, this finding describes what a whole *system* achieves across a population — the aggregate dent in the driveway.

Read together with the roughly **45% of Berlin households that own no car**, it sketches the trajectory the Hub City accelerates: shared access plus dense alternatives normalises car-free living block by block ([[Berlin 45 Percent Car-Free Households]]).

The project's claim is structurally stronger than the studied systems — its sharing is not an add-on competing with abundant parking but the organising element replacing it ([[From Parking City to Hub City]]), paired with supply-side parking removal the literature says is decisive ([[Parking Relocation Effect]]). The 7–15% figure is therefore a floor, not a forecast.
