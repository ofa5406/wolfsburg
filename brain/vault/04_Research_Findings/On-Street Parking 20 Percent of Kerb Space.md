# On-Street Parking 20 Percent of Kerb Space

In the ITF's Lisbon model, on-street parking alone occupies about **20% of kerb-to-kerb street space** — a fifth of the public right-of-way stored under stationary vehicles. Because the shared-fleet scenario removes the need for nearly all on-street parking, that fifth of every street returns to the city at once, without buying a square metre of land ([[ITF Lisbon Study]]).

This finding gives the project its most immediate, most visible payoff surface. Off-street lots and garages convert into hubs and housing over years; the kerb lane converts with paint, planters and bollards in weeks — wider pavements, protected cycle lanes ([[Rad Network]]), loading bays, trees, and the S-hub footprints themselves, which are sized to slot into former parking bays ([[S-Hub]]).

The [[Street Transformation Sections]] draw exactly this dividend street by street: the kerb lane is where the post-car city becomes visible first.
