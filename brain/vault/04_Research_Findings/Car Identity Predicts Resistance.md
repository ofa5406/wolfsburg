# Car Identity Predicts Resistance

The psychological finding that explains why facts alone fail: **car identity, perceived car dependence and "automobility engagement" are strong predictors both of car use and of resistance to reducing it**. For the committed driver, the car is not a transport choice being optimised — it is a self-description being defended, and arguments against driving arrive as arguments against the person.

Regional research deepens the stakes for Wolfsburg: in German car-industry regions, references to local automotive heroes create collective identification between people, place and car — a "**car state**" culture in which acceptance of sustainable mobility drops sharply whenever measures read as restricting personal freedom ([[German Car Culture]], [[Wolfsburg Privileged Identity Study]]). Berlin's stalled car-reduction efforts show the pattern operating even in Germany's least car-dependent city.

The strategic consequence shapes the whole narrative: never fight the identity frontally. The project sells the post-car city as VW's own future demonstrated at home ([[NEW AUTO Strategy]]) and car-light living as the modern urban norm — allowing the identity to migrate rather than demanding it surrender ([[Behaviour Change]]).
