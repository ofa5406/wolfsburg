# Pontevedra

The near-total case — and the repopulation argument. From 1999, Pontevedra's mayor pedestrianised the entire historic centre (~300,000 m²), closed surface car parks, moved parking to free underground and edge lots, replaced traffic lights with roundabouts and imposed 30/10 km/h limits.

Twenty years of results: **traffic in the historic centre down 97%**, through-traffic down ~90% city-wide, CO₂ down ~61–70%, **zero traffic deaths since 2011**, ~70% of trips on foot or bike — and, the number that kills the strongest objection, the centre **gained ~12,000 residents**. Removing cars grew the city rather than emptying it ([[Public Life]]).

For Wolfsburg the structural lesson is the operating model: **edge and underground parking feeding a free interior** — cars intercepted at the perimeter, the core organised for people. That is the hub-at-the-edge logic of the L-hub ring, run for two decades in a mid-sized European city ([[L-Hub]], [[Park-and-Switch Interchange]]). Pontevedra is smaller and denser than Wolfsburg; the brain quotes it for mechanism and morale, not one-to-one transfer.
