# Paris 15-Minute City

The precedent that proves a capital city can do it at scale, now. Under Mayor Hidalgo, Paris spent a decade removing car lanes, building the protected Plan Vélo network (>1,300 km of bike lanes), pedestrianising the Seine banks — and committing to remove **~60,000–70,000 on-street parking spaces, up to 72% of the on-street stock**, with 500 streets voted car-free in 2025 and 45 hectares of new parks ([[Street-Space Reallocation]]).

The behavioural result: **cycling's modal share roughly doubled, ~5% (2020) to over 11% (2025)** — fast, visible shift following protected infrastructure. The political result: Hidalgo expanded the programme amid loud opposition and won re-election by a landslide — deliver, then formalise ([[Imposition Without Consent]]).

Paris also supplies the project's spatial-justice equation — cars carry 13% of trips on 50% of street space ([[Paris 13 Percent Trips 50 Percent Space]]) — and the political brand: [[Carlos Moreno]]'s [[15-Minute City]], proximity as the demand-side complement to the hub network's supply side ([[Proximity Planning]]).
