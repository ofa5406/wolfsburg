# Houten

The filtered-permeability diagram as designed urban form rather than retrofit. Houten, a Dutch new town, was built so the **car network is a ring** feeding internal segments while the **bicycle network is the direct fine-grained grid** through the centre; permeable barriers split the roads into **five segments** — bikes and pedestrians pass, cars must return to the ring ([[Filtered Permeability]]).

Living inside the diagram works: for trips under 7.5 km, **~42% go by bike and ~21% on foot**; residents are measurably more physically active than neighbouring towns and the Dutch average; the town was named Cycling City of the Netherlands 2018.

Houten's value to the project is legitimacy of *form*. Groningen and Ghent prove the retrofit ([[Groningen Traffic Circulation Plan]], [[Ghent Circulation Plan]]); Houten proves the same structure as a deliberate, award-winning typology — a recognised way to build a town, not an experiment imposed on one. Its five segments are the clean visual parallel to Wolfsburg's five zones ([[Five-Zone Model]]), and its tree-branching bike trunk prefigures the [[Rad Network]].
