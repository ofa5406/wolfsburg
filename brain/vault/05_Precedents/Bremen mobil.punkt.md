# Bremen mobil.punkt

The foundational European precedent for a tiered hub network in public space, running since 2003 — and a German peer city, which makes it the project's single most transferable case. Bremen operates **two tiers by vehicle count**: *mobil.punkt* stations (4–12 car-share vehicles, sited at tram/bus/rail with taxi and neighbourhood facilities) and *mobil.pünktchen* (2–3 vehicles dropped into streets with high parking pressure) — the anchor-plus-fine-grain structure the Hub City's three tiers extend ([[Hub Tier System]]).

Scale and outcomes: 51+ stations by 2024, about a third of all car-share stations in Bremen; demand reduction equal to **~32 km of street-space relief**, which the city values against **"significantly over €100 million"** of car-park construction avoided ([[Bremen 16-to-1 Substitution]]).

The governance lesson travels too: providers must meet Blue Angel environmental criteria *and prove parking relief* to earn a station — hub access as regulated public infrastructure, not a franchise ([[Hub as Infrastructure]]). Bremen's Rebecca Karbaumer co-wrote the *Shared Mobility Rocks* planner's guide; the design thinking is documented, citable and one Land away.
