# Freiburg Vauban

Germany's own working proof of car-light living. The Vauban district of Freiburg keeps cars out of its residential streets: households that own one buy a space in an **edge garage for ~$40,000**, unbundled from the home; the streets between are for people. Car ownership runs at **~150 cars per 1,000 residents against ~430 in Freiburg overall** — a third of the city average — and **57% of families who owned a car when they moved in later gave it up**. The system converted its own residents.

Household-level "car-free" figures are reported inconsistently (40–70%); the brain leads with the clean 150-vs-430 comparison.

Vauban is the direct ancestor of the project's spatial grammar: its edge-garage principle is the [[Three-Zone Arrival Sequence]] at district scale — vehicles held at the edge ([[Zone 1 — Vehicle Edge]]), dwelling streets freed ([[Zone 3 — Dwelling Edge]]). And it answers the "not in Germany" objection with a German address: car-light living already works here; Wolfsburg, the capital of the car, is the test of whether it scales ([[Wolfsburg as Testbed]]).
