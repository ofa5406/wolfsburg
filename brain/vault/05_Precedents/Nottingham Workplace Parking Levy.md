# Nottingham Workplace Parking Levy

The precedent for making push measures politically survivable: **hypothecation** — visibly reinvesting the money where the payers can see it. Nottingham charges employers a levy on workplace parking spaces; the revenue **built and runs the city's tram network**. Outcomes 2010–17: car commuting **down 6.6%**, public transport use **up 9.6%**, more than 22% of surveyed commuters switching from car to bus, tram, train or bike.

The mechanism is as important as the numbers. A parking charge whose proceeds vanish into general budgets reads as a tax on drivers; the same charge funding a tram that arrives every seven minutes reads as a trade. People accept losing convenience when they watch it being converted into an alternative ([[Imposition Without Consent]], [[Push and Pull Measures]]).

Wolfsburg's translation: parking revenue and levy logic during the transition years visibly funds the hub network and shift shuttles — every closed parking row pays for the mobility that replaces it, with the ledger public ([[Implementation Timeline]]). The levy also names the natural payer: the employer whose workers fill the spaces ([[Volkswagen]]).
