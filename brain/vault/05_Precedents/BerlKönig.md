# BerlKönig

The cautionary ridepooling precedent — kept in the brain because its failure is load-bearing for the project's economics argument. BerlKönig, run by Berlin's transit authority BVG with ViaVan, pooled up to six passengers per vehicle and delivered genuinely good numbers: **1.85 million passengers over four years**, ~60% of trips replacing car, taxi or car-share journeys, with reduced CO₂ and NO₂ per trip.

It was **shut down in July 2022 anyway** — commercially unviable. The mode shift worked; the business did not, because every vehicle carried a driver whose cost the pooled fares could not cover (succeeded by the smaller *Muva* service).

The lesson the project builds on: **driver cost is what kills dense ridepooling, and removing it is what autonomy is *for***. BerlKönig is the control experiment proving the Hub City's fleet economics need the autonomous transition — which is why the phased-autonomy strategy leans on VW's production vehicle rather than assuming cheap drivers ([[Phased Autonomy]], [[ID Buzz AD Production Level 4]]). Quote it whenever DRT is called "already proven cheap": it is proven *popular*; autonomy makes it *viable* ([[Ridepooling]]).
