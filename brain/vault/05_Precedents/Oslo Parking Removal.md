# Oslo Parking Removal

The precedent for the project's exact mechanism: **attack the parking, not the driver**. Rather than banning cars, Oslo removed **~700–800 on-street parking spaces** inside its inner ring by 2019, replacing them with cycle lanes, benches and parklets, plus targeted street closures.

The traffic followed the parking out: centre car traffic fell 11% (2016–18), then 19% (2018–19), with inner-city areas down 28% overall — and the centre recorded **zero pedestrian or cyclist fatalities in 2019**. Crucially for the objection every retailer raises: **no significant retail revenue loss**, and opposition softened as the results arrived ([[Imposition Without Consent]]).

Oslo demonstrates the causal chain the Hub City is built on: parking supply is the control valve of car use ([[Parking Relocation Effect]]) — remove the spaces, provide the alternative, and traffic evaporates without a single prohibition sign ([[Traffic Evaporation]]). The parklets and benches that replaced the bays are, in kit form, exactly what the S-hub drops into a former parking space ([[S-Hub]]).
