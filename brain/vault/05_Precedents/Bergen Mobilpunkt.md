# Bergen Mobilpunkt

The electric, transit-adjacent core of the hub formula, demonstrated in Norway. Bergen opened the country's first *mobilpunkt* in May 2018 — the name itself borrowed from Bremen ([[Bremen mobil.punkt]]) — and now runs about ten: each combines **reserved, mostly electric car-share bays, secure bike parking, city-bikes and EV charging**, always sited next to public transport and ride-share stops.

Bergen and Oslo developed their networks under the EU Interreg **SHARE-North** programme — the same evidence base and planning community as Bremen — and Oslo's operator Ruter targets a fully emission-free fleet by 2028, confirming the direction of travel: hub networks in the Nordic countries are electric-first by policy, not aspiration ([[Electric Mobility]]).

For Wolfsburg the precedent fixes the non-negotiable core of every M-hub: charging, secure cycle parking and shared EVs at a transit stop ([[M-Hub]], [[Shared EV Bay]], [[Charging Point]]). It also demonstrates diffusion: the Bremen model crossed a sea and a language in fifteen years — evidence the pattern is portable to a third context ([[Hub Tier System]]).
