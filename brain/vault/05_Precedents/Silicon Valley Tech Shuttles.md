# Silicon Valley Tech Shuttles

The proof that employer-anchored mass commuting works at scale — and that big employers will pay for it. Bay Area tech companies (Google, Apple, Genentech) run private shuttle networks totalling **~1,020 commuter buses**; Apple's programme alone cost about **$35 million for ~1,600 employees**. Dedicated shift-worker shuttle operators exist as an established market category serving factories and hospitals ([[Employer-Anchored Commuting]]).

The precedent cuts two ways, and the brain keeps both edges. Positively: where one employer concentrates demand, pooled commuting at scale is routine practice, privately funded as a recruiting tool — Wolfsburg's VW wave is this pattern at maximum intensity ([[VW Shift Wave]], [[Commuter Corridor]]). Critically: the San Francisco tech-bus protests showed what happens when a parallel private system serves the privileged inside public streets — the shuttles became symbols of exclusion.

Wolfsburg's design answers the critique structurally: the shift fleet is not a corporate perk running past the public — between waves, the same vehicles *are* the city's public system, serving residents and visitors ([[Fleet]], [[Equity of the Post-Car Transition]]).
