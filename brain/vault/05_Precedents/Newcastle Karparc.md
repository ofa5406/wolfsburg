# Newcastle Karparc

The consented proof that a multi-storey car park has a second life. Karparc, in Newcastle-under-Lyme (UK), converts the Midway multi-storey into **111 apartments** — one- to three-bed homes plus gym, cinema and residents' lounge — with the exposed concrete frame retained as the building's architecture (design: shedkm).

The wider wave confirms it is a movement, not a one-off: John Lewis plans ~10,000 homes on its store car parks over a decade; SCADpad built micro-apartments inside a working Atlanta garage; Melbourne's Skyfarm turned parking decks into a rooftop farm.

For Wolfsburg's six L-hubs — all converted multi-storey car parks — Karparc is the feasibility citation: the structural frames, floor heights and ramps of the parking city are usable armature, consented and financed in the real market ([[Adaptive Reuse]], [[L-Hub]]). It also completes the thesis poetically: the building type that *symbolised* the parking city becomes the anchor of the post-car one ([[From Parking City to Hub City]]), housing above mobility floors exactly as the L-hub section proposes.
