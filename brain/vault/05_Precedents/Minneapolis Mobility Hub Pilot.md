# Minneapolis Mobility Hub Pilot

The precedent that produced usable numbers from a cheap experiment. In 2019 Minneapolis stood up **twelve hubs for three months** — each a bus stop plus bench, designated bike- and scooter-share parking, and wayfinding signage showing **travel times to real destinations** — layering physical, programmatic and digital components on ordinary street corners.

The findings became siting doctrine: **64% of users said the improvements made them more likely to use the hub's transport options**, and transit-anchored hubs clearly out-performed those placed on bike infrastructure alone — proximity and a *visual* link to the transit stop were decisive ([[Transit-Anchored Hubs Perform Better]]).

Wolfsburg inherits both lessons structurally — every L- and M-hub sits on the trunk network, and the Zone 2 information layer shows journey times, not just maps ([[Real-time Info Terminal]], [[Zone 2 — Threshold]]). The pilot's deeper lesson is methodological: a three-month tactical deployment generated the evidence that justified the permanent programme — trial as research instrument, exactly the phasing logic of the project ([[Tactical Urbanism]]).
