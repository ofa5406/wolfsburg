03 — The cost

<PROBLEMS>

Wide roads, fragmented districts, villages cut off from one another — social disintegration and total dependence on the private car.

Ecological

  • Emissions & air quality
  • Land eaten by parking
  • Fragmented green space
  • Urban heat islands

Social

  • Car-based inequality
  • Core–periphery divide
  • Company-town legacy
  • Unsafe for children & elderly

Urban

  • Monofunctional zoning
  • Car space dominates public space
  • Weak radial transit
  • No intermodal transfer points
03 — The opening

<POTENTIAL>

The very things that make Wolfsburg car-dependent make it ideal for shared autonomous mobility.

Urban form

  • Monocentric → simple hub-and-spoke
  • Compact ~4 km² core
  • Oversized roads = reallocatable slack

Existing infrastructure

  • Ready-made parking stock → depots
  • Dense bus-stop skeleton → last-mile

Industrial / economic

  • VW as anchor + real-world testbed
  • Single employer = predictable demand

Travel demand

  • 76,715 commuters via few corridors
  • ~130,000 residents = pilot scale

04 — The vision

<VISION>

Shared
Accessed on-demand, not owned — frees the land parking now eats, and opens mobility to those who can't or won't own.
Autonomous
No driver cost — frequent, direct service becomes viable even in the low-density periphery.
Electric
Charged at the hubs, centrally managed — zero tailpipe emissions across the city.

05 — Strategy

<STRATEGY>

Reallocation
Every hub sits on infrastructure the city already has — a parking structure, a bus stop. Not one new road.
Hierarchy
Three tiers, one job each — city, district, doorstep. A single mode can't serve a whole city.
Weakest first
Coverage is judged by its weakest district, not its average. The periphery decides success.

05 — How they connect

<CONNECTIONS>

L~4 km
City anchors at the main entries. L↔L runs on the autonomous bus line — the heaviest, longest flows.
L→M~1.5 km
Shuttle + pod hand city-scale traffic down into district-scale flows.
M~2 km
District relays. M↔M (≤1 km) lets neighbours talk directly, without routing back through an L.
M→S~600 m
Pod + e-bike narrow the flow from shared-vehicle scale to human scale.
S~400 m
The final mile at bus stops. S↔S (e-bike) stitches block to block.

06 — What a hub is

<TYPOLOGY>

Ground field

Reddish stone paving that spans the carriageway — a street-section redesign, not a sidewalk add-on.

Vertical beacon

A canopy readable from 50 metres — the hub's signal in the city.

Catalyst, not shelter

Vehicle edge → threshold → dwelling edge. Mobility is the anchor; public life is the ambition.
