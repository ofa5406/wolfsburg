01 · Transport

<PROBLEMS>

Wide roads, fragmented districts, villages cut off from one another. A city of social disintegration and total dependence on the private car.

PLACEHOLDER
Ecological
  • Emissions and air quality
  • Land eaten by parking
  • Urban heat islands
PLACEHOLDER
Social
  • Car-based inequality
  • Core and periphery divide
  • Unsafe for young and old
PLACEHOLDER
Urban
  • Car space dominates public space
  • Monofunctional zoning
  • No transfer points

<POTENTIAL>

The very things that make Wolfsburg car-dependent make it ideal for shared autonomous mobility.

PLACEHOLDER
Urban form
  • Monocentric hub-and-spoke
  • Compact 4 km² core
  • Oversized roads give slack
PLACEHOLDER
Existing infrastructure
  • Parking stock becomes depots
  • Dense bus-stop skeleton
PLACEHOLDER
Industrial / economic
  • VW anchor and testbed
  • One employer, predictable demand
PLACEHOLDER
Travel demand
  • 76,715 commuters, few corridors
  • 130,000 residents, pilot scale

<VISION>

If mobility is reorganized around a shared, multimodal, autonomous system, the land now used for parking becomes available for public life, greenery, housing, and public space.

PLACEHOLDER
Integrated
One system: bus, shuttle, pod, e-bike and EV moving as a network.
PLACEHOLDER
Accessible
Mobility for everyone, not only those who can own and drive a car.
PLACEHOLDER
Social
Reclaimed street space becomes room for public life.

The idea in one line

1Ownership is the problem, not the car.
2Build on what exists: reallocation, not expansion.
3Access is hierarchical: each tier, one job.
4The periphery is not an afterthought.
5Reclaimed street space is the deliverable.
6Decisions follow data, not preference.

<STRATEGY>

Reallocation
Every hub sits on infrastructure the city already has: a parking structure, a bus stop. Not one new road.
Hierarchy
Three tiers, one job each: city, district, doorstep. A single mode can't serve a whole city.
Weakest first
Coverage is judged by its weakest district, not its average. The periphery decides success.

<VISION: HUB>  connection through S · M · L

smaller-scale connectionslarger-scale connections
HUB S
Last-metre nodes that close the coverage gaps the L and M tiers leave.
Sited on bus stops + footfall + bike parking.
HUB M
Intermodal transfer for the dense core: see what's available, then choose.
On existing underground car parks.
HUB L
City-centre anchors and full-fleet operations at the main entry points.
Reuse of multi-storey car parks.

<CONNECTIONS>

L~4 km
City anchors at the main entries. L↔L runs on the autonomous bus line, the heaviest, longest flows.
L→M~1.5 km
Shuttle + pod hand city-scale traffic down into district-scale flows.
M~2 km
District relays. M↔M (≤1 km) lets neighbours talk directly, without routing back through an L.
M→S~600 m
Pod + e-bike narrow the flow from shared-vehicle scale to human scale.
S~400 m
The final mile at bus stops. S↔S (e-bike) stitches block to block.
